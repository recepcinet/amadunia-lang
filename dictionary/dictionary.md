## Counting to a hundred

These are **not roots** and are not counted in the 113. Every one is built from the roots above by the rule in [grammar/numbers.md](../grammar/numbers.md) — the digit, then *des*. Learn eleven words and you can count to a hundred.

| | Amadunia | built from |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | des | *(root)* |
| 20 | du-des | du + des |
| 30 | tri-des | tri + des |
| 40 | pat-des | pat + des |
| 50 | fai-des | fai + des |
| 60 | sis-des | sis + des |
| 70 | seti-des | seti + des |
| 80 | ba-des | ba + des |
| 90 | nau-des | nau + des |
| 100 | sen | *(root)* |

The teens work the other way round — *des* first, then the digit: 11 = *des-uan*, 12 = *des-du*, 19 = *des-nau*. Anything in between joins the two: 21 = *du-des-uan*, 99 = *nau-des-nau*, 345 = *tri-sen pat-des fai*.

There is not one irregular number anywhere in the system.

## A collision, resolved

*luma* (light) and *ruma* (house) differed only in *l* versus *r* — the hardest contrast on Earth for over a billion speakers, and the ground on which *beli* and *cala* had been rejected. *luma* is the founder's word; *ruma* was the first batch's, added before the rule existed. On September 3, 2026 *ruma* became **dom**, and the dictionary now contains no l/r pair at all.
