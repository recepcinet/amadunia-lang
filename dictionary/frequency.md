# How often each root is actually used

Generated from the material a learner reads — the twenty-five lessons, the
eight texts and the phrasebook — counting every word inside an Amadunia
sentence and ignoring the vocabulary tables, which teach a word rather than use
it. **4257 words of running Amadunia**, and every one of the 300 roots appears
at least once.

the numbers follow the corpus rather than the other way round.

Ten roots carry a third of everything written. That is steeper than a natural
language and it is what a small dictionary looks like from the inside: the
grammar words — *mi*, *yu*, *ta*, *es*, *in*, *ini*, *suda*, *no*, *aur* — do
most of the work, and the nouns take turns.

For a learner the practical reading is the second row: **twenty-five roots
cover half of everything.** They are the first twenty-five below.

## Used exactly once

37 roots appear in a single sentence in the whole corpus:

*amani*, *arte*, *bai*, *banka*, *bas*, *bola*, *fai*, *grupo*, *hi*, *historia*, *kaki*, *kirim*, *legis*, *legum*, *madad*, *mati*, *mersi*, *mila*, *minyak*, *namak*, *natura*, *numero*, *ok*, *pardon*, *plis*, *posta*, *proba*, *risi*, *sabun*, *sansi*, *sapatu*, *sofer*, *start*, *stop*, *tair*, *ya*, *yatak*

Being used once is not a fault — a word for *snow* will not turn up often in
eight texts about tea, hospitals and letters. It is a list of what the material
has not yet had a reason to say, and it is where the next text should look.
