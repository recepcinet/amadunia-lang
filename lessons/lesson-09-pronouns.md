# Lesson 9 — We, you all, they

*Prerequisite: [Lesson 8](lesson-08-getting-by.md).*

## New words

None. Every form in this lesson is built from words you have.

## Say it twice

You know *mi*, *yu*, *ta*. You know that saying a word twice makes it "more than one". Put those together:

| one | more than one |
|---|---|
| mi — I | mi-mi — we |
| yu — you | yu-yu — you all |
| ta — he, she, it | ta-ta — they |

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Ta-ta suda lai. | They came. |
| Yu-yu mau ca? | Do you all want tea? |
| Mi-mi rabota sini. | We work here. |
| Dom ta-ta nali? | Where is their house? |

## Two kinds of "we"

You also know *kita*. It means "we" too — but a particular we.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| mi-mi | we, **not** you | me and my people |
| kita | we, **with** you | you and me, together |

> — Mi-mi go market sasa. Yu-yu?
> — No, mi-mi sini.
> — Ok. Kita saufa kula una rat!

*We're going to the market now. You all? — No, we're staying here. — Ok. We'll all eat together tonight!*

The last line uses *kita* because the speaker means everyone — both groups.

## One word, three jobs

*ta* is he, she, **and it**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Doktor lai. Ta hao. | The doctor is coming. She is good. |
| Ca garam. Mi mau ta. | The tea is hot. I want it. |
| Anak keci. Ta suda dormi? | The child is small. Has he slept? |

## Nothing changes

The same pronoun whether it does the action or receives it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi kan ta. | I see her. |
| Ta kan mi. | She sees me. |
| Yu-yu bil mi-mi? | Do you all know us? |
| Mi-mi bil yu-yu. | We know you all. |

No *I / me*, no *she / her*, no *we / us*. One form each.

## A conversation

> — Salam! Yu-yu es kim?
> — Mi-mi es rafiki Sol. Sol sini?
> — Ya, ta sini. Ta-ta rabota.
> — Ta-ta es kim?
> — Sol, rafiki ta, anak-anak ta. Kita go kan ta-ta?
> — Ya! Mersi!

*Hello! Who are you all? — We're Sol's friends. Is Sol here? — Yes, she's here. They're working. — Who are they? — Sol, her friend, her children. Shall we go see them? — Yes! Thanks!*

## Practice

1. Ta-ta mau akua. — *They want water.*
2. Yu-yu suda kan doktor? — *Did you all see the doctor?*
3. Mi-mi saufa lai kab? — *When will we come?* (we, not you)
4. Kita saufa lai sasa. — *We'll come now.* (all of us)
5. Nama ta-ta ke? — *What are their names?*
6. Pan garam. Mi mau ta! — *The bread is hot. I want it!*

## What you can already say

Nine lessons, 76 roots. You can now talk about anyone — one person or many, with you or without you, a person or a thing — and the words for them have never once changed their shape.
