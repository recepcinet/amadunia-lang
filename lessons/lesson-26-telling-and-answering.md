# Lesson 26 — Telling and answering

*Prerequisite: [Lesson 25](lesson-25-doing-and-being.md).*

No new words. Two rules that this course has been using since Lesson 1 and
Lesson 10 without ever teaching them, because until September 3, 2026 neither
had been granted: **the command**, and **the one-word answer**. Both are in
[sentence-types.md](../grammar/sentence-types.md), and both cost the language
nothing — no word was added and no word changed shape.

## A command is a verb with no subject

| | |
|---|---|
| Otur sini. | Sit here. |
| Buka pintu. | Open the door. |
| Espera momen. | Wait a moment. |
| Lai dom mi. | Come to my house. |
| Dengar mualim. | Listen to the teacher. |
| Kirim surat por mama yu. | Send a letter to your mother. |

That is the whole rule. Amadunia never leaves a subject out of a statement, so
a sentence that begins with a verb can only be one thing.

## The subject is what makes it a statement

| | |
|---|---|
| Go! | Go! |
| Yu go. | You go. |
| Kula! | Eat! |
| Yu kula pan. | You eat bread. |

*Yu go* is a statement about you, however loudly it is said. Putting the
pronoun back "for emphasis" would make one sentence mean two things, and this
language does not do that. Emphasis is the voice and the mark: **Go!**

## Saying no to a command

*no* goes in front of what it denies, exactly as it does in a statement.

| | |
|---|---|
| No go. | Do not go. |
| No kula nyama itu. | Do not eat that meat. |
| No lala sasa. | Do not sleep now. |
| No kimbia hayai. | Do not run fast. |

The order is *no* → verb → adverb, which is the order you already know.

## *plis* is politeness, not grammar

| | |
|---|---|
| Otur, plis. | Sit, please. |
| Beri akua, plis. | Give water, please. |
| Madad, plis. | Help, please. |

Take *plis* away and the sentence is still a command. Add it and nothing
changes grammatically — which is why a warning does not take it: *No go!*

## One word is an answer

A phrase may be a whole turn when what it answers is clear.

> — Yu mau ke?
> — Ca.

> — Kab?
> — Besok.

> — Nali?
> — In dom.

> — Yu suda kula?
> — Ya.

The last one means *yes, I ate*. The tense, the subject and the verb all come
from the question; nothing is repeated and nothing is marked.

## A command and an answer can be the same word

> — Yu mau suru ke?
> — Kula.

*Kula* at the start of an exchange is **eat!**; *Kula* answering a question is
**eating**. Context does the work, and English does the same with *Eat.*

## A conversation

> — Mama, mi bimar.
> — Otur sini. Buka mulut.
> — Ya.
> — Yu bimar. No go skola besok.
> — Mi mau go!
> — No. Lala sasa. Mi saufa leta ca aur ilac.
> — Mersi, mama.

*Mother, I'm ill. — Sit here. Open your mouth. — Yes. — You are ill. Don't go
to school tomorrow. — I want to go! — No. Sleep now. I'll bring tea and
medicine. — Thank you, mother.*

Seven of those eleven sentences are commands or one-word turns — four commands (*Otur sini*, *Buka mulut*, *No go skola besok*, *Lala sasa*) and three turns of one word.

## Practice

1. Buka pencere, plis. — *Open the window, please.*
2. No nomu akua itu. — *Do not drink that water.*
3. Lai sini aur otur. — *Come here and sit.*
4. — Yu mau ca o kafe? — Ca. — *Do you want tea or coffee? — Tea.*
5. Kara hao libro ini. — *Read this book well.* — the adverb stays with its verb; *Kara libro ini hao* would be "read this good book".
6. Yu kara libro ini. — *You read this book.* — a statement, because it has a subject.

## What this lesson could not do

**There is no way to tell someone to *be* something.** *Be quick*, *be
careful*, *be a doctor* have no form: a sentence-initial *es* is already the
existential, so *Es doktor* is "there is a doctor". The mother above says *Otur
sini* and *Lala sasa* because she cannot say *be still*.
[That is the open question the command rule left](../grammar/sentence-types.md),
and [text 12](../texts/text-12-tarik-por-skola.md) wanted it three times on one
page.
