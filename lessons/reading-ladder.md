# The reading ladder

*How much of the writing you can read after each lesson.*

Derived from the material itself and regenerated by [`check.py`](../check.py),
so it follows the course rather than a claim about it. The measure is plain:
every word inside an Amadunia sentence in [the twenty-nine texts](../texts/),
counted with repeats, against the words the lessons have taught by that point.

**Nearly a quarter of everything after one lesson — 23%.** That figure has
been 23, then 22, then 23 again in three days, printed four lines above a table
that is regenerated: it moves whenever a text moves the denominator, and it
moved up when [text 28](../texts/text-28-din-mi.md) and
[text 29](../texts/text-29-yu-mau-ke.md) were written inside the first eight
lessons.
[The front page](../README.md) had the same number as *a quarter*, with no
figure at all — rounding 22 up by three on the page a reader meets first. Both
are computed from this table now. That is the
frequency curve doing the work — [ten roots carry a third of the
corpus](../dictionary/frequency.md), and the first lesson teaches the commonest
of them. Half by Lesson 06, three quarters by Lesson 16.

**Five lessons leave the figure unchanged: 09, 11, 17, 23 and 26.** Four of
them teach no new root at all — pronouns, the copula, a review and the two
sentence rules are grammar, not vocabulary. Lesson 17 teaches two, *bisa* and
*lasim*, and they are too few to move a whole point. Nothing is wrong with any
of the five; the measure cannot see what four of them add and rounds away what
the fifth does.

**This list has now changed five times in five days, and no lesson has changed
once.** Lessons 05 and 17 have been on it and off it repeatedly as the texts
moved the denominator underneath them: 05 left when
[text 28](../texts/text-28-din-mi.md) was written inside the first six lessons,
and 17 came back the next day when [text 29](../texts/text-29-yu-mau-ke.md) was
written inside the first eight. A lesson's figure on this page is a fact about
the corpus as much as about the lesson — the same reason **Half by Lesson 06**
replaced *Lesson 07* above, which had replaced *Lesson 08*, which had replaced
*Lesson 07*, without any of those lessons being touched. The list is derived
and will keep moving; what is worth reading is the four that teach no root, and
those four do not move.

**The last stretch is steep because it was built that way.** Lessons 24 and 25
were written to sweep up the roots that had reached the dictionary without a
lesson, so they carry 94% to 100% between them.

**[Din mi](../texts/text-28-din-mi.md) is the first thing a learner can read,
at Lesson 06** — 30 roots and five adjectives, with no conjunction, no
negation, no question word, no copula and no preposition at all. It was written
after this table showed that the course taught for nine lessons before it
offered anything to read.

**[Yu mau ke?](../texts/text-29-yu-mau-ke.md) is the second rung, at Lesson
08** — 37 roots, and a conversation rather than a description: Lesson 07 hands
over the question words and almost every line of it is a question or an answer
to one.

**[Sol lai](../texts/text-15-sol-lai.md) is the third rung, at Lesson 10** —
89 roots, no prepositions, no *ini* or *itu*, no verb chain and no *porke*. It
held the bottom of this table until text 28 was written, and it was itself
written when the bottom was Lesson 13.

**[Story 1](../texts/story-1-anak-espera-sol.md) comes next, at Lesson 13** —
written at 113 roots against Lessons 1–13, which is why it opens early.

**[In skola](../texts/text-20-in-skola.md) is the fifth rung, at Lesson 16**,
written for the five lessons between 13 and 18 that had nothing new to read.

**[Yamur aur ca](../texts/text-14-yamur-aur-ca.md) is the sixth rung, at
Lesson 18,** and it exists because this page was measured. The first version of
this table had story 1 at 13 and then nothing until 24: eleven lessons with no
new reading. It was written inside the 147 roots the course has taught by
Lesson 18, to put a rung in the middle of that gap, and *Sol lai* was written
for the gap below it.

**[Kamra mi](../texts/text-19-kamra-mi.md) is the seventh rung, at Lesson 21**,
written for what was then the largest gap left in this table — six lessons
between 18 and 24 with nothing new to read.

Everything else waits for Lesson 24 or 25, because every other text was written
at 300 roots. That is a fact about how the texts were made, not about how hard
they are: [*Uan*](../texts/text-5-uan.md) uses 21 roots and
[*Kaifa suru ca*](../texts/text-8-kaifa-suru-ca.md) uses 19, but each reaches
for one word that arrives late.

**Knowing every word is not the same as understanding the sentence.** A text
needs the rules as well, and the two do not have to arrive together — the verb
chain does not come until Lesson 17, existence and comparison until 18. **Each
row is the later of the two**, so it is what a reader actually needs.

For twenty-eight of the twenty-nine texts the two halves make no difference:
the last word a text needs arrives at or after its last rule, and the widest
margin is [text 17](../texts/text-17-tren-aur-farasi.md), which needs nothing
after Lesson 15 and waits until 25 for a word.

**[Text 28](../texts/text-28-din-mi.md) is the one exception, and it is why
this page changed.** Every word in it is taught by Lesson 04 and it uses
possession, which is Lesson 06. Until September 6, 2026 this table held the
vocabulary figure alone, with a separate check that no text's grammar had
overrun it — a check that passed because no text ever had. It would have
offered text 28 three lessons early. One number, computed from both halves,
and [`check.py`](../check.py) requires this page to name any text where they
differ.
