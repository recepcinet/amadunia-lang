# Lesson 10 — A day

## Here and there

*sini* has its pair now.

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi otur sini. | I sit here. |
| Yu otur situ. | You sit there. |
| Hotel nali? — Situ. | Where is the hotel? — There. |
| Familia mi situ. | My family is there. |

## Doing things

| | |
|---|---|
| Yu suru ke? | What are you doing? |
| Mi suru rabota. | I'm working. — *literally: I do work* |
| Mi ambil telefon. | I take the telephone. |
| Ta suda kupi pan. | She bought bread. |
| Kita saufa kupi ca. | We'll buy tea. |

## Telling someone to do it

Leave the subject out and the sentence becomes a command. Nothing is added.

| | |
|---|---|
| Otur sini. | Sit here. |
| Kula pan. | Eat bread. |
| No go! | Do not go! |

*Yu otur sini* is different — it is a statement about you. A command has no
subject at all, and that is what makes it one. [The rule](../grammar/sentence-types.md).

## The body

| | |
|---|---|
| Sar mi garam. | My head is hot. |
| Ain ta kabir. | Her eyes are big. — *or:* her eye is big |
| Mano-mano anak keci. | The child's hands are small. |
| Mi kan yu. Ain mi hao. | I see you. My eyes are good. |

*ain* on its own is neutral — one eye or two, as the situation makes obvious. *ain-ain* when the "two" matters.

## A day, start to finish

> Din. Mi ambil telefon. Mi suru rabota.
> Mi go market. Mi kupi pan. Mi kupi ca. Mi kupi akua.
> Mi lai dom. Familia mi situ. Kita kula una.
> Rat. Mi otur. Mi lala.

*Day. I take the phone. I work. I go to the market. I buy bread. I buy tea. I buy water. I come home. My family is there. We eat together. Night. I sit. I sleep.*

Every verb in that story is in its one and only form. Not one of them changed for *I*, for *we*, for past or present.

## A conversation

> — Pardon, hotel nali?
> — Situ. Yu-yu es familia?
> — Ya, mi-mi es familia. Anak-anak mi-mi saufa lala.
> — Ok. Hotel hao. Yu-yu saufa lala.
> — Mersi!

*Excuse me, where is the hotel? — There. Are you a family? — Yes, we're a family. Our children will sleep. — Ok. The hotel is good. You'll sleep. — Thanks!*

## Practice

1. Yu lala kab? — *When do you sleep?*
2. Mi suda kupi telefon. — *I bought a telephone.*
3. Otur sini, plis. — *Sit here, please.*
4. Sar ta mal? — *Is his head bad?* (does his head hurt?)
5. Familia yu situ? — *Is your family there?*
6. Ta-ta suru ke sasa? — *What are they doing now?*

## What you can already say

Ten lessons, **93 roots** — past the first milestone of 80. You can now tell the story of a whole day, name the body, point here and there, buy and take and make and sleep.

**A note on word order.** *situ*, like *sini* and *sasa*, sits at the end of its sentence — place before time. This became the rule in [Lesson 15](lesson-15-pointing-placing.md).
