# Lesson 15 — Pointing and placing

*Prerequisite: [Lesson 14](lesson-14-not-and.md).*

## New words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ini | this |
| itu | that |
| in | at, in, on |
| dari | from |
| por | to, for |
| o | or |

Six little words. After this lesson the grammar you need to get through a day is complete.

## This and that

They go after the noun, and after everything else in the phrase:

| | |
|---|---|
| dom ini | this house |
| anak itu | that child |
| Ca ini garam. | This tea is hot. |
| Insan itu es doktor. | That person is a doctor. |
| dom mi kabir ini | this big house of mine |

On their own they are a subject, and before a noun they take *es*:

| | |
|---|---|
| Ini es dom mi. | This is my house. |
| Itu es ke? | What is that? |
| Ini hao, itu mal. | This is good, that is bad. |

You have met their shape before: *s-ini* is here, *s-itu* is there.

*din ini* is today. *rat ini* is tonight.

## At, from, for

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi in dom. | I am at home. |
| Anak lala in dom. | The child sleeps in the house. |
| Mi lai dari market. | I come from the market. |
| Yu dari nali? | Where are you from? |
| Mi beri pan por yu. | I give bread to you. |
| Ca ini por kim? — Por mi! | Who is this tea for? — For me! |

*por* is the *por* in *porke* — "for what", why.

Going somewhere needs no word at all. After *go, lai, anda, kimbia*, the place is the destination:

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi go market. | I go to the market. |
| Ta lai dom. | She comes home. |

## Or

| | |
|---|---|
| Ca o akua? | Tea or water? |
| Mi mau pan o ca. | I want bread or tea. |
| Yu lai din ini o din itu? | Are you coming today or that day? |

## With

*una* was "together". Put a noun after it and it is "with":

| | |
|---|---|
| Kita kula una. | We eat together. |
| Kita kula una familia. | We eat with the family. |
| Mi go market una rafiki mi. | I go to the market with my friend. |

## Answering *no*-questions

| | |
|---|---|
| — Yu no mau ca? | Don't you want tea? |
| — No. | I don't. |
| — Ya. | I do. |

*ya* and *no* answer the **fact**, never the shape of the question. *Ya* means it is so; *no* means it is not. When it matters, answer with the verb: *Mi mau.* / *Mi no mau.*

## Where the little phrases go

Place first, then time, both at the end:

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi kula pan in dom din ini. | I eat bread at home today. |
| Kita saufa lai dari market rat ini. | We'll come from the market tonight. |

## A conversation

> — Salam! Yu dari nali?
> — Mi dari dom. Yu go nali?
> — Mi go market. Yu lai una mi?
> — Ya! Mi mau pan o ca.
> — Pan ini hao. Pan por familia yu?
> — Ya, por familia mi. Aur ca por mi!
> — Kita go sasa. Market situ, kiri.

*Hello! Where are you from? — From home. Where are you going? — To the market. Coming with me? — Yes! I want bread or tea. — This bread is good. Bread for your family? — Yes, for my family. And tea for me! — Let's go now. The market is there, on the left.*

## Practice

1. Dom ini kabir aur dom itu keci. — *This house is big and that house is small.*
2. Mi lai dari market una dugu mi. — *I come from the market with my sibling.*
3. Ca ini por yu o por mi? — *Is this tea for you or for me?*
4. Anak-anak lala in dom rat ini. — *The children sleep at home tonight.*
5. Yu no faham? — Ya, mi faham. — *Don't you understand? — Yes, I do.*
6. Ini es ke? — Itu es telefon. — *What is this? — That is a telephone.*

## What you can already say

Fifteen lessons, **122 roots**. You can point at things, place them, say where they came from and who they are for, offer a choice, go somewhere with someone, and answer any question without ambiguity. That is the grammar of A1, and it is done.
