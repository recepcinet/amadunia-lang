# Lesson 5 — More than one

*Prerequisite: [Lesson 4](lesson-04-tense.md).*

## New words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| cok | many, much |

One word. The rest of this lesson needs no new vocabulary at all.

## Say it twice

To say "more than one", say the noun twice with a hyphen between.

| One | More than one |
|---|---|
| anak | anak-anak |
| dom | dom-dom |
| rafiki | rafiki-rafiki |
| doktor | doktor-doktor |

Nothing is added. Nothing inside the word changes. There is no word in Amadunia this does not work on.

## In a sentence

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Anak-anak kula pan. | The children eat bread. |
| Mi kan dom-dom. | I see houses. |
| Rafiki-rafiki suda lai. | The friends came. |
| Doktor-doktor saufa sema. | The doctors will speak. |

## When you already said how many

The numbers are on [the front page](../README.md), and no lesson teaches them,
because there is nothing to teach: twelve words with not one exception between
them. This is where they are first used.

If there is a number, do not double the noun. The number already told everyone.

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| tri anak | three children |
| du dom | two houses |
| cok rafiki | many friends |
| des doktor | ten doctors |

So: *anak-anak* **or** *tri anak* — never *tri anak-anak*.

## When you do not need it at all

A plain noun is neither singular nor plural — it is just the thing. *Mi kan anak* is "I see a child" or "I see children", whichever is obvious from where you are. Double the noun only when the "more than one" actually matters and nothing else in the sentence says it.

## A conversation

> — Yu suda kan anak-anak?
> — Ya. Tri anak. Anak-anak keci!
> — Rafiki-rafiki lai?
> — Cok rafiki saufa lai.

*Did you see the children? — Yes. Three children. The children are small! — Are the friends coming? — Many friends will come.*

## Practice

1. Anak-anak nomu akua. — *The children drink water.*
2. Mi kan du sol? — *Do I see two suns?*
3. Rafiki-rafiki suda kula. — *The friends ate.*
4. Cok din, cok rat. — *Many days, many nights.*
5. Doktor bil dom-dom. — *The doctor knows the houses.*

Five lessons, **59 roots**. Past, present, future; one thing or many; and a grammar in which no word has ever changed its shape.
