# Lesson 3 — People, day and night

*Prerequisite: [Lesson 2](lesson-2-food-and-home.md).*

## Talking about people

The sentence shape has not changed since Lesson 2 — subject, verb, object.

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Mi kan rafiki. | I see a friend. |
| Anak nomu akua. | The child drinks water. |
| Ta bil nama. | She knows the name. |
| Rafiki sema. | The friend speaks. |

## Describing

A quality word follows what it describes:

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Ruma kabir. | The house is big. |
| Anak kechi. | The child is small. |
| Cha hao. | The tea is good. |
| Rat mal. | The night is bad. |

There is no separate word for *is*. *Ruma kabir* is complete as it stands.

## Day and night

> — Din hao?
> — Ya, din hao. Sol kabir!

> — Rat hao?
> — No. Rat mal.

## Asking for help

> — Salam! Mi mau doktor.
> — Ok. Doktor lai.

## Practice

1. Mi kan anak. — *I see a child.*
2. Rafiki nomu cha. — *The friend drinks tea.*
3. Doktor bil. — *The doctor knows.*
4. Ruma mi kechi. — *My house is small.* — **careful:** possession has no settled rule yet; see below.
5. Din kabir, rat kechi. — *The day is long, the night is short.*

## What is still open

Sentence 4 above is a guess, not a rule. How to say *my*, *your*, *their* has not been decided — putting the pronoun after the noun is only one option. The same goes for:

- **Plurals** — how to say *children* rather than *child*
- **Questions** — whether a particle replaces the rising tone used since Lesson 1

These are founder decisions. Until they are made, every lesson uses singular nouns. Tense has since been settled — see [Lesson 4](lesson-4-tense.md).
