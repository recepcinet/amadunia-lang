# Possession

*Status: settled — decided September 2, 2026. Reversible if the founder disagrees; see "Why this and not the alternatives" below.*

## The rule

**The owner comes right after the thing owned. Nothing is added.**

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| ruma mi | my house |
| nama yu | your name |
| anak ta | her child, his child |
| rafiki kita | our friend |
| ruma rafiki | the friend's house |
| nama anak | the child's name |

No possessive pronouns to learn (*my, your, his*), no suffix, no "of". The ordinary pronoun does the job, and so does any ordinary noun.

## Where it sits

The owner takes the slot immediately after the noun — the same slot an adjective takes. When both are present, the owner comes first, then the adjective:

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| ruma mi kabir | my big house |
| anak ta kechi | her small child |
| rafiki kita hao | our good friend |

*ruma mi* is a unit: "my house". Whatever follows describes that unit.

## Chains

An owner can itself have an owner. Each one follows what it owns, and the chain can go as long as it needs to:

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| ruma rafiki mi | my friend's house — *house (of) friend (of) me* |
| nama anak rafiki yu | your friend's child's name |

Read left to right, each word belongs to the one before it. No exception at any depth.

## In a sentence

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Mi kan ruma yu. | I see your house. |
| Anak ta suda nomu akua. | Her child drank water. |
| Rafiki mi saufa lai. | My friend will come. |
| Kita bil nama doktor. | We know the doctor's name. |

## Sources

Placing the owner straight after the noun, with no marker, is how a large share of the world already does it:

| Family | Example |
|---|---|
| Austronesian | Indonesian/Malay *rumah saya* (house I), *nama dia* (name he) |
| Semitic | Arabic *bayt-u r-rajul* (house the-man), Hebrew construct state |
| Iranian | Persian *khāne-ye man* (house-of me) — with a linking vowel, same order |
| Niger-Congo | Swahili *nyumba yangu* (house my) — owner after noun |
| Celtic | Welsh *tŷ Siôn* (house John), Irish |
| Tai-Kadai, Austroasiatic | Thai *bâan khǎw* (house he), Vietnamese *nhà tôi* (house I) |
| Romance | *la casa de Juan* — noun first, with *de* |

It also matches what Amadunia already does with adjectives: modifiers follow the noun.

## Why this and not the alternatives

**Why no marker.** Every marker candidate with real reach collides with a word already settled: Chinese *de* 的 is a minimal pair with *des* (10); Japanese *no* の is already "no"; Swahili *ya* is already "yes". That three independent candidates all collide is a sign the language does not want a marker here — and juxtaposition needs none.

**Why owner before adjective.** The owner is the most identifying information ("whose house"); adjectives describe. Arabic and Indonesian, the two largest noun-first models, both bind the owner tighter to the noun than the adjective.

**Why not separate possessive pronouns (*my, your*).** A second pronoun set doubles what a learner must memorise and, in every language that has one, drifts away from the base set (English *I/my*, Spanish *yo/mi*). Reusing the ordinary pronoun means there is nothing extra to learn and nothing that can drift.

**Candidates rejected, and why:**

| Candidate | Source | Reason rejected |
|---|---|---|
| de (particle) | Chinese *de* 的, Romance *de* | minimal pair with *des* (10) |
| no (particle) | Japanese *no* の | already "no" |
| ya (particle) | Swahili *-a* class agreement | already "yes" |
| -im / -in (suffix) | Turkish, Hungarian, Arabic | inflection on the noun — against the spirit of rule 3 |
| owner first (*mi ruma*) | Chinese, Japanese, Turkish, Hindi, English | would be the only modifier placed before the noun — one exception to an otherwise clean pattern |

## Open questions

- **Attributive vs predicate.** *Ruma mi kabir* can mean "my big house" or "my house is big". This ambiguity is not created by possession — *ruma kabir* already has it — but possession makes it more visible. Whether the language wants a copula or a linking particle is a separate decision.
- **Alienable vs inalienable.** Some languages distinguish "my hand" from "my house". Amadunia currently does not; whether it ever should is open.
