# Proposal: where a frequency adverb stands

**Nothing here is adopted.** This page measures what the material actually
does, sets out the three answers and what each would cost, and leaves the
decision to the founder.

It matters more than its size suggests: *kadang* is one of only two roots in
the dictionary that [cannot be written at all](../dictionary/proposal-a2.md),
because any sentence using it would settle this question by accident.

## The record said one thing and the corpus says another

[adverbs.md](adverbs.md) records that *daima* and *kadang* "sit in the adverb
slot with everything else". Counted on September 4, 2026 across the lessons,
the texts, the grammar files and the phrasebook, **two of thirteen do**.

| Position | Uses | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **before the verb** | 5 | *Mualim daima kara libro* · *Ta kadang lai* · *Mi kadang go in tren* |
| **the adverb slot** | 2 | *Mi kula daima pan* · *Anak proba daima* |
| **after the object** | 6 | *Mi nomu halib daima* · *Mi ingat yu daima* · *Anak-anak kula telur kadang* |

Thirteen sentences, three positions, and one lesson — Lesson 19 — uses all
three by itself. Nobody chose this; the hand followed whichever source language
it was thinking in.

One of the six depends on a question that is also open: *Kita rabota daima in
skola* counts as "after the object" only because
[*rabota* is a noun in the dictionary](verb-chains.md). Read it as a verb and
that sentence moves into the adverb slot. Two open questions touching one
sentence is worth knowing before either is answered.

## The three answers, costed

| | Rule | Sentences to rewrite | Argument for | Argument against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | the adverb slot: *Mi kula **daima** pan* | 11 of 13 | one rule for every adverb; [adverbs.md](adverbs.md) already says so | the most rewriting, and it puts the commonest use — the bare *Ta kadang lai* — in the position it is least often written |
| **B** | before the verb, where the tense markers stand: *Ta **kadang** lai* | 8 of 13 | frequency is about time, and *daima kara* would sit where *suda kara* sits | a second word before the verb, so the order of *no*, *suda* and *daima* has to be settled too |
| **C** | last in the sentence, like *tena*: *Mi nomu halib **daima*** | 7 of 13 | cheapest, and it matches the one neighbour that has already settled itself | the tail of a sentence is where [place and time](place.md) already go, and a fourth thing there is a crowd |

## *tena* settled itself and nobody noticed

*tena*, "again", is not a frequency word — it counts repetitions rather than
naming a rate — but it is the nearest neighbour in the dictionary and it has
been consistent from the start: **23 of its 23 uses are last in the sentence**,
without one exception. *Mi lai tena*, *Anak katab tena*, *Yamur no lai tena*,
*Din lai tena*.

No rule ever granted that. The writing simply never wavered, which is the
opposite of what happened with *daima*, and it is the strongest evidence
available for answer **C** — the language has already been doing it with the
word next door.

## What is measured and what is judgement

Measured: the thirteen sentences and their three positions, the cost of each
answer, and *tena*'s 23 of 23 — [`check.py`](../check.py) recounts all of them,
so this page cannot drift the way [adverbs.md](adverbs.md) did.

Judgement, and the founder's: which position, whether *daima* and *kadang*
behave alike, and whether *tena* is bound by the same answer or stays a
separate thing that happens to sit in the same place.
