# Word formation

*The two joinings are taught in [Lesson 05](../lessons/lesson-05-plural.md) and on [the front page](../README.md); nothing else joins.*

**Settled: two roots may be joined in exactly two ways, and both were settled
elsewhere. There is no third way, and no compound word exists.**

| Joining | Means | Where it was settled |
|---|---|---|
| *des-uan*, *du-des-uan* | eleven, twenty-one | [numbers.md](numbers.md) |
| *anak-anak*, *dom-dom* | children, houses | [plural.md](plural.md) |

## What the corpus actually contains

Every hyphenated form in the repository was collected on September 3, 2026 —
lessons, texts, grammar files, dictionary and front page. There are 35 of
them, and every single one is a number or a reduplicated plural:

- 16 number shapes: *des-du*, *du-des*, *ba-des*, *du-sen*, *du-mila* …
- 19 reduplications: *anak-anak*, *ain-ain*, *doktor-doktor*, *hora-hora* …
- nothing else.

**Not one compound word has ever been formed.** The language has no *airport*,
no *bookshop*, no *railway* — no word built out of two words.

## What has been called compounding is not compounding

The front page has explained the 300-root target by saying compounding does
the rest, and [text 6](../texts/text-6-seti-din.md) called *mesin ambil foto
korpo anak* "the compounding the design was betting on". That sentence means
*a machine takes a picture of the child's body*, and it does say X-ray without
a new root — but it is a **sentence**, not a word. Nothing was formed. What
carried it was the possession rule and a verb.

That distinction matters for the vocabulary argument. Three hundred roots go
further than three hundred words because things can be **described** with what
is already there, not because new words can be **built**. Description is a
weaker claim than word-formation, and it is the true one.

## Open questions

- **Does the language form compound words at all?** It never has, and the
  front page has been leaning on the idea that it does. The decision has two
  hard parts. First, **juxtaposition is already taken**: *foto korpo* cannot
  mean "body-photo" as one word, because the possession rule already makes it
  "the body's picture". Second, the hyphen is already doing two jobs, and a
  third would make *des-uan*, *anak-anak* and a compound three unrelated
  things wearing one mark. Whatever the answer, it decides how far 300 roots
  really reach, so it belongs with [the A2
  briefing](../dictionary/proposal-a2.md). Found by scanning every hyphen in
  the repository after noticing that "compounding" appeared four times and had
  no page.
