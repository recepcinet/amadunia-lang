# Proposal: marking a name

**This is not a rule.** Nothing here has been adopted. It is the briefing for
the third of the four questions the language is already relying on without
permission, after [stress](proposal-stress.md) and
[the imperative and the fragment](proposal-sentence-types.md).

## The problem, measured

Two dictionary roots are used as people's names throughout the material:
**Sol** and **Luma**, which are also the words for *sun* and *light*.

This page first said Sol was used as a name 33 times and Luma 18. Those figures
cannot be reproduced, and the reason is the problem itself: separating a
name-use from a sun-use is a judgement, not a measurement, and it is exactly
the judgement no rule currently supports. Counting every occurrence instead
gives Sol 51 and Luma 19.

**Forty-one sentences are formally ambiguous.** That number *is* reproducible,
and [`check.py`](../check.py) now recounts it: a sentence whose first word is
*Sol* or *Luma*, where every word in it is a dictionary word and nothing says
which reading is meant. Ten are in each of the two stories, four in Lesson 12,
three in Lesson 11, and the rest scattered across ten more files.

It was thirty-nine until September 4, 2026, when
[text 15](../texts/text-15-sol-lai.md) was written for Lesson 10 and opened
with *Sol lai* — the sun comes, or Sol comes. The text was not written about
this question and reached the ambiguity twice on its own, which is the
strongest argument on this page: the collision is not in the old material, it
is in the words.

| | Reading A | Reading B |
|---|---|---|
| Sol hao. | Sol is good. | The sun is good. |
| Sol kabir. | Sol is big. | The sun is big. |
| Sol asfar. | Sol is yellow. | The sun is yellow. |
| Sol es doktor. | Sol is a doctor. | The sun is a doctor. |

And the same string means different things in different texts.
[Story 1](../texts/story-1-anak-espera-sol.md) writes *Sol beri luma* four
times meaning **the sun gives light**;
[Story 2](../texts/story-2-safari-por-pahar.md) writes *Sol sema* meaning **a
child named Sol says**. Nothing in the language distinguishes them.

## Why capital letters are not the answer

They are what the material uses today, and they work on a page. But design
rule 2 is **written as spoken**, and a capital letter cannot be spoken. A
listener hearing *sol hao* has nothing at all.

This is the only open question where the status quo contradicts one of the
five founding rules directly.

## Why it cannot be avoided by choosing better names

The obvious dodge — say a name may not be an existing root — does not survive
contact with the world. A world auxiliary language has to carry the name of
anyone who has one, and people are called Sol, Luna, Kanta, Mira and Nadia.
The dictionary has 300 roots today and will have a thousand; every name will
eventually collide with something.

**The problem gets worse as the language succeeds.**

## The options

**A personal article — a small word before the name.**
This is a real and widespread device, not an invention: Tagalog *si*, Portuguese
*o/a*, Catalan *en/na*, Māori *a*, Fijian *o*, Ancient Greek, southern German.
Roughly it would be *X Sol hao* — "Sol is good" — with *X* saying that what
follows is a name.

Cost: one word, and it must survive the minimal-pair rule, which has killed
every two-letter candidate the language has tried. It would probably have to be
three or four letters.

**Capitals only — keep the status quo and say so.**
Cost: nothing, but it leaves the language unspeakable at exactly the point
where people introduce themselves, and it breaks rule 2.

**Nothing — let context do it.**
Chinese and English manage. But they manage partly because their name stock is
recognisable as names, and Amadunia's is not: *Sol* is a perfectly ordinary
word of the language.

**A shape rule for names.**
Names must end in a sound no root ends in, or take a fixed ending. Cost: it
mangles the name, which is the one thing a person will not accept about their
own name.

## What a decision needs to say

Whether the marker appears with every name or only where ambiguity is possible;
whether it also marks place names (*Amadunia* itself is a name and appears
unmarked five times in [text 3](../texts/text-3-lingua-ini.md)); whether it is
stressed; and what happens to the thirty-six sentences already written — most
of which, on inspection, would simply gain one word.

## The other question still waiting

*"Want to be" plus an adjective* has not been briefed yet. It is the smallest
of the four: *es* is a verb and chains freely, but an adjective is its own
predicate and takes no verb, so *mau* has nothing to attach to and "wants to be
the easiest" cannot be built. Both [text 3](../texts/text-3-lingua-ini.md) and
Lesson 21 hit it and were rewritten around it. Recorded in
[copula.md](copula.md).
