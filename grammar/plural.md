# Plural

*Taught in [Lesson 05](../lessons/lesson-05-plural.md).*

*Status: settled — decided September 2, 2026. Reversible if the founder disagrees; see "Why this and not the alternatives" below.*

## The rule

**To make a noun plural, say it twice, joined by a hyphen.** Nothing is added, nothing changes inside the word.

| Singular | Plural | English |
|---|---|---|
| anak | anak-anak | children |
| dom | dom-dom | houses |
| rafiki | rafiki-rafiki | friends |
| din | din-din | days |

That works on every noun in the language, with no exceptions, because there is nothing that could go wrong: no ending to choose, no sound to change, no list of irregulars.

## After a number, the noun stays single

If the sentence already says how many, the noun is not doubled.

| | |
|---|---|
| tri anak | three children |
| du dom | two houses |
| cok rafiki | many friends |

The number has done the work; repeating it would be saying the same thing twice. This is the same principle as tense: once the information is there, it is not restated. (*Anak-anak* on its own means "children, more than one, count unspecified".)

## Where plurals are not needed

The unmarked noun is neutral, not singular. *Mi kan anak* means "I see a child" **or** "I see children" — whichever the situation makes obvious. Doubling is used when the plural actually matters and nothing else in the sentence shows it.

## Sources

Doubling a word to mean "more than one" is one of the most widespread devices on Earth:

| Family | Example |
|---|---|
| Austronesian | Indonesian/Malay *anak-anak* (children), *rumah-rumah* (houses); Tagalog |
| Sino-Tibetan | Chinese *rén-rén* 人人 (everyone), *tiān-tiān* 天天 (every day) |
| Japonic | Japanese *hito-bito* 人々 (people), *yama-yama* 山々 (mountains) |
| Dravidian | Tamil, Telugu — distributive and plural reduplication |
| Afro-Asiatic | Somali, Hausa — plural by partial reduplication |
| Turkic | Turkish keeps nouns single after a number (*üç çocuk*, never *üç çocuklar*) — the second half of this rule |

Keeping the noun single after a numeral is itself shared by Turkish, Hungarian, Persian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Indonesian.

## Why this and not the alternatives

**Why not a suffix (-s, -ler).** A suffix is easy while it is regular, and in every natural language that has one it stops being regular: English *child/children*, Spanish *lápiz/lápices*, Arabic broken plurals. Doubling cannot drift because there is no form to drift.

**Why not a particle (like *suda* for tense).** A plural particle placed before the noun would behave exactly like an article, and design rule 3 says no articles. Placed after the noun it would be one more word to learn and one more position rule; doubling needs neither.

**Why not "no plural at all" (the Chinese model).** Tempting, and it is why the unmarked noun is neutral here. But learners coming from languages with obligatory plurals need *some* way to say "more than one" when it matters. Doubling gives them one without making it compulsory.

**Candidates rejected, and why:**

| Candidate | Source | Reason rejected |
|---|---|---|
| -s | English, Spanish, French, Portuguese | Indo-European only; breaks rule 4 for a feature this basic |
| -ler | Turkish | one family; and as a separate particle it has no meaning to a non-Turkish speaker |
| men | Chinese *-men* 们 | in the source it works only on people and pronouns — borrowing it as a general plural would misrepresent Chinese |
| tachi | Japanese *-tachi* たち | same problem: people only |
| duo (many) | Chinese *duō* 多 | introduces the vowel sequence *uo*, not attested in the language |

## Open questions

- **All, some, none.** *cok* covers "many" and stands before the noun — *cok
  rafiki*. There is nothing for *all* or *some*. **"None" may already be
  covered**: the existential negation settled in
  [copula.md](copula.md) gives *No es ca*, there is no tea, which is what "none"
  usually means in a sentence — so the question is narrower than its name, and
  is really about *all* and *some*. Nothing written so far has tried to say
  either, which is the whole of the evidence for it. Recorded here, where the
  decision would live, on September 3, 2026.
