# Place and role — in, dari, por

*Status: settled — decided September 3, 2026.*

## Three small words

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **in** | at, in, on | Mi **in** dom. — I am at home. |
| **dari** | from | Mi lai **dari** market. — I come from the market. |
| **por** | to, for | Mi beri pan **por** yu. — I give bread to you. |

Each goes **before** its noun. *in* turns a noun into a place; *dari* into a source; *por* into a recipient or a purpose.

## Going somewhere needs nothing

After a verb of motion — *go, lai, anda, kimbia* — the place that follows is the destination. No word is added:

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi go market. | I go to the market. |
| Ta lai dom. | She comes home. |
| Anak kimbia market. | The child runs to the market. |

This has been true since Lesson 2. It is now the rule: **a motion verb's object is where it is going.** Chinese does exactly this — *wǒ qù shìchǎng*, "I go market".

## In

| | |
|---|---|
| Anak lala in dom. | The child sleeps in the house. |
| Kita kula in market. | We eat at the market. |
| Sol in dunia. | The sun is in the world. |

Place words — *sini, situ, upar, sub, kiri, yamin* — are already places and take no *in*. *Mi sini*, never *mi in sini*.

## Dari

| | |
|---|---|
| Ca ini dari dugu mi. | This tea is from my sibling. |
| Yu dari nali? | Where are you from? |
| Akua dari yuki. | Water from snow. |

## Por

| | |
|---|---|
| Ca ini por kim? — Por mi! | Who is this tea for? — For me! |
| Ta rabota por familia ta. | She works for her family. |
| Mi kupi pan por anak-anak. | I buy bread for the children. |

*por* was already in the language, folded inside *porke* — "for what", why. Now it stands on its own.

**Why giving needs *por*.** *Mi beri pan dugu mi* would read, by the possession rule, as "I give my sibling's bread". *por* keeps the recipient apart from the owner: *Mi beri pan por dugu mi*.

## With

*una* means "together". Put a noun after it and it means "with":

| | |
|---|---|
| Kita kula una. | We eat together. |
| Kita kula una familia. | We eat with the family. |
| Mi go market una rafiki mi. | I go to the market with my friend. |

No new word. *una* is Latin *ūnā* — the founder's word — and in Latin it already meant both "together" and, with *cum*, "together with".

## Where these phrases go

**Place first, then time, both at the end of the sentence.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Mi kula pan in dom. | I eat bread at home. |
| Mi kula pan din ini. | I eat bread today. |
| Mi kula pan in dom din ini. | I eat bread at home today. |
| Kita saufa lai dari market rat ini. | We will come from the market tonight. |

This settles a question every lesson since 7 had left open: *sini, situ, sasa, kab, porke, kaifa* all go last, and now the rule says so.

## Sources

| Word | Source |
|---|---|
| in | Latin *in*, English *in*, German *in*, Italian *in*, Dutch *in* — one of the few words shared unchanged across the whole Indo-European west |
| dari | Indonesian and Malay *dari* |
| por | Spanish and Portuguese *por*, *para*; Italian *per*; French *pour*; Latin *pro* |

Three words for at, from and to is the floor. Indonesian has exactly these three (*di, dari, ke*); so does Turkish (*-da, -dan, -a*), Arabic (*fī, min, ilā*), and Toki Pona (*lon, tan, tawa*). Fewer than three and a language leans on its verbs; Amadunia does that for "to" and no further.

## Why prepositions, not suffixes

Amadunia is subject-verb-object with modifiers after the noun. Languages built that way — Indonesian, Swahili, Thai, Vietnamese, Arabic, Spanish — put their place words *before* the noun, and languages built the other way — Turkish, Japanese, Hindi — put them after. The language follows its own shape.

Nearly every short candidate collided:

| fi | at (Arabic) | minimal pair with *mi*, *hi* |
| ni | at (Japanese) | minimal pair with *mi*, *hi* |
| da | at (Turkish) | minimal pair with *ta*, *ba*, *ca*, *ya* |
| en | at (Spanish) | minimal pair with *es* |
| me | in (Hindi) | minimal pair with *mi*, *ke* |
| min | from (Arabic) | minimal pair with *din* |
| de | from (Spanish) | minimal pair with *du*, *ke* |
| se | from (Hindi) | minimal pair with *ke* |
| ot | from (Russian) | minimal pair with *ok* |
