# Proposal: where the stress falls

**This is not a rule.** Nothing in the language depends on it yet and nothing
here has been adopted. It is the briefing for the decision that
[the open questions](README.md) put first, laid out so it can be made in one
sitting.

## Why this is first

[The poem](../texts/text-5-uan.md) showed what the absence costs. Read its
opening line stressing *DIN lai*, then *din LAI*: both are correct Amadunia and
they are different poems. Two people cannot agree on the metre of a single
line, so nothing can be sung, scanned, or taught by ear. Every other open
question limits what can be *said*; this one limits what can be *spoken*.

## A prior question that has to go first

You cannot say "stress the second-to-last syllable" until you have said what a
syllable is. In 28 of the 300 roots — 9% — the count depends on whether an
attested vowel pair is one nucleus or two:

| | one nucleus | two |
|---|---|---|
| *dunia* | du-nia (2) | du-ni-a (3) |
| *akua* | a-kua (2) | a-ku-a (3) |
| *mausim* | mau-sim (2) | ma-u-sim (3) |
| *familia* | fa-mi-lia (3) | fa-mi-li-a (4) |

The affected roots are the ones containing *ai, ao, au, ia, ua*: *ain, akua,
auto, bai, dunia, familia, fai, kimbia, lai, mau, mausim, mualim, naiti, nau,
sungai, tair* and twelve others.

**This is now recorded as an open question in [phonology.md](phonology.md).**
It has to be settled first, or settled at the same time.

## The options

**Penultimate — the second-to-last syllable.**
Esperanto, Swahili, Indonesian and Malay, Polish, Quechua. Two of Amadunia's
three largest source families already do this, and so does the one constructed
language that has actually been spoken for a century.

**Initial — always the first.**
Czech, Hungarian, Finnish, Icelandic, Latvian.

**Final — always the last.**
Turkish, Persian, French, Armenian, Hebrew.

**None — leave it free.**
What the language does today. It is why the poem has no metre.

## The finding that makes this easier than it looks

**For 209 of the 300 roots, penultimate and initial are the same syllable.**
A two-syllable word has its first syllable as its penultimate one. The two
options can only disagree on the 34 three-syllable roots — *bahari, habari,
kalima, kamisa, kucing, madina, muskil, negara, numero, polisi, restoran,
safari, samaki, sapatu, telinga, uhuru* and the rest.

So the practical choice is between penultimate-or-initial on one side and final
on the other, and even that separates only a third of the vocabulary.

## And it is free

**No two words in Amadunia are told apart by stress, and none ever can be** —
that would be a minimal pair, which the language forbids. Whatever is chosen,
no existing word changes meaning and no text has to be rewritten. Nothing is at
risk.

That is exactly why it has been easy to defer for a day, and exactly why it
should be settled now: the cost of choosing is nil and the cost of not choosing
is that the language cannot be spoken in metre.

## What a decision needs to say

Following the shape of the other files in this directory: the rule in one
sentence; whether a vowel pair is one syllable or two; which languages already
do it and how many people that is; the options rejected and why; and what it
leaves open — clitics, whether *mi-mi* is stressed once or twice, and whether
the grammar particles *es, no, aur, o, in, dari, por* carry stress at all.
