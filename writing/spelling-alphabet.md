# Spelling alphabet

*Status: proposed — drafted September 3, 2026. Not a decision; see "Why these words" below.*

## Why these words

**Every one is already in the dictionary.** Nobody learns a second vocabulary to use
this. NATO had to invent *Alfa* and *Xray*; Amadunia had 180 roots to choose from and
needed none of them changed. A speaker who knows the language knows the code.

**No two can be mistaken for each other.** No pair differs by a single letter, and no
two end in the same three letters — the two failures that make a spelling alphabet
useless in noise. Every word is four to seven letters, so none is short enough to be
swallowed.

**The families are spread.** Arabic 3 (*insan, madina, salam*), Indic 3 (*garam,
upar, negara*), Latin and Romance 4 (*akua, espera, familia, libro*), already-global
4 (*banka, hotel, kafe, taksi*), Turkic 2 (*otur, yamur*), French 1 (*pardon*),
Swahili 1 (*rafiki*), Amadunia-internal 2 (*cang, dunia*). Design rule 4 holds.

**Most are concrete nouns.** A thing is easier to hear and repeat than an action or a
quality. Where the letter left no choice — *c*, *o*, *u*, *e* have few words each —
the best available was taken.

## Words considered and not taken

| Letter | Candidate | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| a | anak (child) | *-nak* clashes with *namak* (salt), and *anak* is the language's most-used example word — a code word should be neutral |
| m | mama (mother) | minimal pair with *nama* (name); *m* and *n* are exactly the pair a spelling alphabet exists to separate |
| n | nama (name) | same pair, from the other side |
| p | papa (father) | *b*/*p* is the second-worst pair on a bad line, and *papa* against *banka* is too little distance |
| y | yuki (snow) | shorter and lighter than *yamur*; both are Turkic, so nothing was lost |
| h | historia, hospital | good words, but long enough to be clipped by a bad connection |
| k | kita (we) | a pronoun, and *ki-ta* is close to *ta* said twice |

## Open questions

- **The letters with almost no choice.** *c* has three words in the language, *o* and
  *u* have three each. If any of those letters gains a better word later, the code
  word should change with it — this table is not meant to be frozen.
- **Digits.** Numbers are read as ordinary Amadunia numerals; whether *fai* (5) and
  *sis* (6) need distinguishing on a bad line has not been tested.
- **Nobody has used this yet.** Like the [keyboard](keyboard.md), it is computed and
  reasoned, not proven in the air.
