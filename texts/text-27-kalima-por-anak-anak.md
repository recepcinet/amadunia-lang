# Kalima por anak-anak

*Words for the children.*

The twenty-seventh text, and the first **notice** — a page written to be pinned
on a wall and read by everybody, rather than said by somebody to somebody.

It was drafted claiming to be the only page in the corpus with nobody speaking
and nobody addressed, and that was not measured and is not true. **Four texts use
no *mi* at all**: this one, [the recipe](text-8-kaifa-suru-ca.md), and the
portraits in [text 6](text-6-seti-din.md) and
[text 11](text-11-anak-katab.md). The recipe is the nearest thing to a notice
already here, and what separates them is who *yu* is — the recipe's *yu* is one
cook following one method, and a notice's *yu* is whoever happens to be
standing in front of the wall. The language spells those the same way, which is
the finding this page was written to reach and not the one it set out for.

A notice is made of commands and of the word *everyone*, and the language has
the first and not the second. This page was written to find out how far the
first alone gets.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 26.

---

```
Kalima por anak-anak in skola.

Lai in pagi. Buka pintu.
Buka libro yu. Kara.
Dengar mualim.

No kimbia in kamra. Kimbia in bage.
No kula in kamra. Kula in bage.

Leta libro aur kertas. Leta akua.
No leta kanis.

Agar yu no faham, tanya.
Agar yu bimar, sema mualim.

Gasil mano yu. Gasil piring yu.
Funga pintu kab yu go.

Skola buka in pagi. Skola funga in aksam.
Kita studi una.
```

## What a notice is made of

**Fifteen of the twenty-one sentences are commands**, and three of those are
negative. A notice is the genre the imperative was granted for, and it did not
exist when the imperative was granted:
[the briefing](../grammar/proposal-sentence-types.md) counted seven commands in
the whole material and one text written deliberately without any.

The other six are the title, three statements about the school itself, and the
two conditionals. **Both conditionals speak to one reader**, which no command
here does: *Agar yu no faham, tanya* and *Agar yu bimar, sema mualim* single
out the child who does not understand and the child who is ill, and each is
fronted with a comma, which is the one fronting the rules grant.

## Gaps

**There is no word for everyone, and a notice is addressed to everyone.** The
page says *yu* throughout — the same *yu* that means one person — and only the
title, *kalima por anak-anak*, says who is meant. *all*, *some* and *none* are
[an open question](../grammar/plural.md) and none was invented here; without
them a public notice cannot say it is public.

**No word for late or early.** The first line wants *do not come late* and says
*come in the morning*, which is a different instruction: it tells a punctual
child something they already know instead of telling a late one anything.
*late* and *early* are both [missing from the checklist's time
domain](../dictionary/a1-checklist.md).

**A notice wants a numbered list and cannot have one.** *First, second, third*
have no form — [ordinals are open](../grammar/numbers.md) — so the instructions
stand in the order they are printed and nothing says that the order matters.
For *come in the morning* before *close the door when you go* it does.

**And there is no word for a rule.** *legis* is law, which is a courtroom, and
*kalima* is a word; neither is what a notice is made of, and *a rule* now
stands on [the gap list](../dictionary/README.md) with the rest. The title says
*words for the children* because *rules for the children* cannot be said, and a
notice that cannot name what it is is relying on the wall it is pinned to.
