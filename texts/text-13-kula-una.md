# Kula una

*Eating together.*

The thirteenth text, and the first written **for *una***. Counted across the
lessons, the texts and the phrasebook on September 4, 2026, *una* stood in 21
sentences — fewer than any other settled rule, and fewer than half of what the
adverb rule had before [text 11](text-11-anak-katab.md) was written for it.

*una* does two jobs. Alone after a verb it is "together"; with a noun after it,
it is "with". Of the 21, seventeen were "with" and **four** were "together", so
the rarer of the two jobs is what this page mostly does.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 26.

---

```
Aksam. Familia mi in dom.

Mama tabak corba una legum.
Papa gasil piring una sabun.
Mi leta pan dari market una dugu mi.

Rafiki mi lai. Kanis ta lai una ta.
Kita otur una.
Kita kula una.

Corba garam. Pan baru.
Mama sema: "Kula, plis."
Kita kula aur sema aur kanta.

Anak keci lala in kursi.
Papa leta ta por yatak.

Rat. Dom sukut.
Mi senang porke kita una.
```

## What *una* did

**Seven of the seventeen sentences use it**, and between them they cover both
jobs and three kinds of "with" that English keeps apart:

| | |
|---|---|
| una legum | an ingredient |
| una sabun | an instrument |
| una dugu mi, una ta | a companion |
| otur una, kula una, kita una | together |

The last row is the one that needed writing. Four sentences in the whole
corpus had used the bare adverb; this page adds three more and one of them,
*Mi senang porke kita una*, uses it with no verb at all — the shape the copula
rule licenses, and the shape that ["Mi una yu"](../lessons/lesson-22-doing-and-feeling.md)
was corrected into on the same day.

## Gaps

**With an object, the two jobs of *una* collide.** *Kita kula una* is "we eat
together", and *Kita kula una pan* should be "we eat together, bread" — but it
reads as "we eat with bread", because *una* followed by a noun is the
preposition. There is no way to say *we eat the bread together* in one
sentence. This page avoids the collision by keeping every *together* sentence
free of an object, which is a way of writing around a hole, not a solution.
[Recorded as an open question in place.md](../grammar/place.md).

**No word for *but*.** *Corba garam. Pan baru.* would rather be "the soup was
hot **but** the bread was old", which is the sentence a shared meal actually
produces. [The ninth gap the writing has asked for](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for),
found the same day in Lesson 12.

**No word for *then*.** The child falls asleep and is carried to bed; the order
on the page is again doing what the grammar cannot.
