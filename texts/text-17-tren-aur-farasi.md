# Tren aur farasi

*The train and the horse.*

The seventeenth text, and the second written for **the roots no text had ever
used**. [Text 16](text-16-berapa-harga.md) took the market words and the
function words and brought the count from 55 down to 34. This one goes to the
country after the rest.

A journey was the obvious shape, because most of what was left is out there:
a train, a taxi, a farmer, a horse, a cat, a garden, stones, a key, a shirt.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 26.

---

```
Uan mensis suda go. Mi suda punya mimpi: safari kabir.

Mi ambil taksi. Sofer leta mi por tren.
Tren anda hayai. Tren anda pat hora.

Mi kan negara mi. Petra kabir in pahar. Asman asul.

Nasab mi espera sini. Ta es kisan.
Cehra ta hao. Rambut ta siya.
Ta buka pintu una anahtar.

Ta punya farasi aur kucing keci.
Animal-animal in bage. Bage sub, dom upar.

Mi beri regalo por ta: kamisa asul.
Ta senang. Mi senang.

Kita otur. Ta sema habari negara.
Kita dengar musik. Musik ini es kultura ta.

Rat lai. Mi lala. Mi punya mimpi tena.

Besok mi saufa go dom mi.
Tarik duan aur safari no muskil.
Mi fikir uhuru es safari.
```

## What it reached

**Twenty-seven roots used here for the first time in any text**: *anahtar*,
*animal*, *asul*, *bage*, *cehra*, *duan*, *farasi*, *habari*, *kamisa*,
*kisan*, *kucing*, *kultura*, *mensis*, *mimpi*, *musik*, *muskil*, *nasab*,
*negara*, *pat*, *petra*, *rambut*, *regalo*, *safari*, *sub*, *taksi*, *tren*,
*uhuru*. The draft called it sixteen; the list under it had twenty-seven in it,
and the count is the thing that was wrong.

**Seven roots are now left**, and two of them cannot be used at all yet:
*madad*, whose class is undecided, and *kadang*, whose position as a frequency
adverb is an open question. Writing either would answer a question this text
has no standing to answer. The other five are *burun*, *dekat*, *gusa*, *kiri*
and *polisi*.

*sub* was the one that needed care. It is a place word, like *sini* and *situ*,
not a preposition: *Bage sub* is "the garden is below", and *sub dom* would be
inventing a fourth preposition beside *in*, *dari* and *por*. The line was
written the way the language allows and reads better for it.

*pat*, four, was the last digit no text had used. It is in *Tren anda pat
hora* — four hours — which also leaves duration unmarked, the way
[Lesson 13](../lessons/lesson-13-weather-directions.md) already does with *din
cang*.

## Gaps

**No word for a station.** (Not on [the list](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for) and not in [the A1 checklist](../dictionary/a1-checklist.md) either — a station is a thing neither method had reached.) The taxi brings me *por tren*, to the train, because
there is nowhere for a train to be. Every journey text will hit this.

**Duration has no marker.** *Tren anda pat hora* is "the train goes four hours",
and nothing distinguishes that from a time when. The language has been doing
this since Lesson 13 and has never decided it; it belongs beside
[the question about *in* and time](../grammar/place.md).

**No word for *but*, a fourth time.** *Tarik duan aur safari no muskil* wants
"the road is short **but** the journey is not easy". Every text that has
tried to contrast two clauses has wanted it —
[the ninth gap](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for), now
asked for by four pages.
