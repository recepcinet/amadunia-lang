# Pagi in madina

*A morning in the city.*

The ninth text, and the first written from a list rather than from an idea.
[The frequency page](../dictionary/frequency.md) ended by noting that
thirty-seven roots appear in exactly one sentence in the whole corpus, and that
this is where the next text should look. Eighteen of them are here.

An errand is the right shape for it: a bed, soap, shoes, a driver, the post
office, the bank, rice and salt do not belong to one theme, but they belong to
one morning.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 23.

---

```
Pagi lai. Mi start din.
Mi go dari yatak. Mi gasil mano mi una sabun.
Mi pone sapatu in kaki mi.

Mi anda por tarik. Tair kanta in asman.
Auto lai. Sofer sema: "Yu go nali?"
Mi respon: "Mi go posta."

Mi kirim surat in posta.
Mi go banka. Mi ambil pesa.

Mi kupi risi, legum, minyak aur namak in market.
Mi beri fai pesa.

Grupo anak-anak suru oyun una bola in tarik.
Mi lihat ta-ta. Mi senang.

Mi lai dom. Mi stop anda. Din finis.
```

## What the text found

**The commonest words did not get commoner.** The point of writing from a
frequency list is to reach the tail, and it worked: eighteen roots that had one
use each now have two. But *mi* still opens thirteen of the twenty-one sentences,
because a first-person errand cannot avoid it. The distribution is a fact about
the language, not about the material.

**A verb pair carried the frame.** *Mi start din* and *Mi stop anda* open and
close the day. *start* and *stop* are already-global loans that had been sitting
unused since Lesson 25 taught them, and they turn out to do exactly what they
do everywhere else.

**One morning holds words from six themes.** A bed, soap, a shoe, a foot, a
driver, the post, a bank, rice, oil, salt, a bird, a ball. A thematic wordlist
separates those; a life does not. That is an argument for writing texts rather
than filling categories, and it is the same argument the vocabulary gaps made
from the other side — [the words the writing asked for](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for)
were never the words a category would have suggested.

## Gaps

**It cannot say "I got up".** *Mi go dari yatak*, I go from the bed, is a
paraphrase of a position change the language has no verb for. Rising, standing,
sitting down — *otur* is to sit, and there is nothing for its opposite.

**It cannot count money.** *Mi beri fai pesa* is "I give five money"; whether
*pesa* pluralises, and what a coin is as against an amount, is untouched.
[The clock could not be asked either](../phrasebook.md), and this is the same
kind of hole: the language names things well and measures them poorly.

**"Along the road" is *por tarik*, and that is a guess.** *por* is the
preposition for *to, for*; using it for a path is not something any rule
grants. [Place](../grammar/place.md) settles direction and location and says
nothing about a route, and the second tier of prepositions — through, without,
about — is recorded there as being for later. This sentence is the first to
want one.
