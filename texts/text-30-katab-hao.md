# Katab hao

*Write well.*

The thirtieth text, and the fourth rung on [the reading
ladder](../lessons/reading-ladder.md): **only the roots the lessons have taught
by Lesson 12**, and no rule beyond that lesson.

The ladder ran 06, 08, 10 and then nothing until 13. This page was written for
the two lessons in that gap, and for what Lesson 12 is actually about: an
adjective straight after the verb describes the verb. *Katab hao* is **write
well**, and *katab hao* is the whole point of the page.

Twelve lessons in there is no *aur*, no *no* before a predicate, no *ini* or
*itu*, no preposition, no verb chain and no comparison. What there is that the
earlier rungs did not have: the command, the copula, and the adverb.

---

```
Anak mi keci. Mano ta keci.

Otur sini. Ambil mano mi.
Kan mano mi. Mi katab.

Katab keci. Katab hao.
Anak mi katab hayai.

Mi sema: "Katab hao."
Ta katab hao.

Ta katab nama mama.
Ta katab nama papa.
Ta katab nama ta.

Ta kara sasa. Ta kara hayai.
Ta kara hao.

Anak mi es rafiki mi.
Ta saufa katab hao.
```

## Line by line

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Anak mi keci. Mano ta keci. | My child is small. Her hand is small. |
| Otur sini. Ambil mano mi. | Sit here. Take my hand. |
| Kan mano mi. Mi katab. | Look at my hand. I write. |
| Katab keci. Katab hao. | Write small. Write well. |
| Anak mi katab hayai. | My child writes fast. |
| Mi sema: "Katab hao." | I say: "Write well." |
| Ta katab hao. | She writes well. |
| Ta katab nama mama. | She writes mother's name. |
| Ta katab nama papa. | She writes father's name. |
| Ta katab nama ta. | She writes her name. |
| Ta kara sasa. Ta kara hayai. | She reads now. She reads fast. |
| Ta kara hao. | She reads well. |
| Anak mi es rafiki mi. | My child is my friend. |
| Ta saufa katab hao. | She will write well. |

## The one rule this page is for

**An adjective after the verb describes the verb; an adjective after a noun
describes the noun.** Nothing is added and nothing changes shape, so the same
two words mean two things depending on which side of the verb they fall:

| | |
|---|---|
| Anak mi katab hayai. | My child writes fast. |
| Anak hayai katab. | The fast child writes. |

Both are on this page's own terms — the second is not written above, because
the child in it is not a fast child. That is the whole of Lesson 12, and a text
is where a rule stops being a table.

**The command is what makes it a lesson rather than a description.**
**Five of the nineteen sentences are commands**, and zero of those are
negative — *Otur sini*, *Ambil mano mi*, *Kan mano mi*, *Katab keci*, *Katab
hao*. That is what teaching a child to hold a pen sounds like in any language,
minus the half of it that says *don't*.

## Gaps

**There is nothing to write on and nothing to read.** *kertas*, paper, arrives
in Lesson 24 and *libro*, book, in Lesson 21, so a page about learning to write
has no pen, no paper and no book in it. The child writes and reads, and the
objects of both verbs are missing from the language a learner has at this
point. [The reading ladder](../lessons/reading-ladder.md) says what is
readable; it does not say what is writable, and this is the difference.

**And *Ta katab nama ta* cannot say whose name it is.** *nama ta* is "her
name", and nothing marks whether that is her own name or somebody else's — a
reflexive is [an open question](../grammar/pronouns.md) and none was invented
here. The line is meant as her own, and the page cannot say so.

**Nothing can be joined and nothing denied.** *aur* and predicate *no* are both
[Lesson 14](../lessons/lesson-14-not-and.md), which is why *Katab keci* and
*Katab hao* stand as two orders where a parent would say one. The same gap
[text 29](text-29-yu-mau-ke.md) recorded at Lesson 08 is still open four
lessons later, and it is the longest-running gap on the low rungs.

**And a child cannot want to do anything.** The first line of this page read
*Ta mau katab*, she wants to write, until [`check.py`](../check.py) refused it:
two verbs in a row is a verb chain and
[the chain is Lesson 17](../grammar/verb-chains.md). *mau* takes a noun at this
rung — [text 29](text-29-yu-mau-ke.md) says *Mi mau ca* five lessons earlier —
so wanting a thing is sayable and wanting to do a thing is not. The line is
*Mano ta keci* now, her hand is small, which is a fact about the child rather
than a wish of hers.
