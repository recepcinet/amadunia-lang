# Anak katab

*A child writes.*

The eleventh text, written for one rule. [The rules are all exercised
somewhere](README.md), but the adverb — an adjective straight after the verb,
describing the verb — appeared in one text out of ten. It is also the rule
[the lessons used from Lesson 10 and did not introduce until Lesson
12](../grammar/adverbs.md), so it has been the least examined thing in the
language twice over.

Manner is what a lesson about learning to write is made of. This is that
lesson, told rather than taught.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 23.

---

```
Mualim katab hao. Anak lihat.
Anak proba katab. Anak katab mal.

Mualim sema asan: "Yu proba tena."
Anak katab tena. Mano ta no kuat.

Anak studi kuat. Anak proba daima.
Anak katab hao sasa.

Anak kara hao libro ta.
Mualim lihat kertas. Mualim senang.
Mualim sema: "Yu suda studi hao."

Anak kanta hayai. Anak kimbia dari skola.
Anak katab nama ta in kertas baru.
```

## What the text found

**One rule carries the whole story — eleven times in eighteen sentences.**
*katab hao*, *katab mal*, *katab tena*,
*studi kuat*, *kanta hayai*, *kara hao libro* — the same adjective-after-verb
in every one, and the story is the difference between *mal* and *hao*. Nothing
is added to make an adjective describe a verb: the position does it, and the
word does not change.

**The extension earns its keep in one line.** *Anak kara hao libro ta* — the
adverb comes before the object, which is the part [Lesson
18](../lessons/lesson-18-comparing-and-joining.md) adds to the rule. Without
it, *kara libro hao* would read as a good book rather than reading well, and
the sentence would be about the book.

## Gaps

**There is no word for *slowly*.** The child writing badly is the point of the
first half, and the natural sentence is that the child writes *slowly* rather
than badly, which is not the same thing and not unkind in the same way.
*hayai* is fast and has no opposite. The text used *mal*, bad, which says more
than it means to.

**It cannot say "better than before".** *lebi hao* is better, and comparing it
to an earlier state needs *dari* with something to point at — a moment rather
than a thing. The text says *Anak katab hao sasa*, the child writes well now,
and lets *sasa* carry the change.
