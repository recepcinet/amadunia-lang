# Uan

*One.*

The fifth text, and the first that is not prose. The stories tell, the third
text argues, the fourth is two people talking. This one is meant to be said
aloud, which turns out to be the thing the language is least ready for.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 23.

---

```
Din lai. Sol upar.
Rat lai. Bulan upar.
Din lai tena.

Mi anda in tarik.
Yu anda in tarik.
Kita anda in uan tarik.

Mi lihat sol.
Yu lihat sol.
Kita lihat uan sol.

Mi punya kalima.
Yu punya kalima.
Kita punya uan lingua.

Mi ama dunia.
Yu ama dunia.
Kita ama dunia una.
```

---

## Line by line

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Din lai. Sol upar. | Day comes. The sun is above. |
| Rat lai. Bulan upar. | Night comes. The moon is above. |
| Din lai tena. | Day comes again. |
| Mi anda in tarik. | I walk on the road. |
| Yu anda in tarik. | You walk on the road. |
| Kita anda in uan tarik. | We walk on one road. |
| Mi lihat sol. | I see the sun. |
| Yu lihat sol. | You see the sun. |
| Kita lihat uan sol. | We see one sun. |
| Mi punya kalima. | I have words. |
| Yu punya kalima. | You have words. |
| Kita punya uan lingua. | We have one language. |
| Mi ama dunia. | I love the world. |
| Yu ama dunia. | You love the world. |
| Kita ama dunia una. | We love the world together. |

## What the design gives a poem for free

**Parallelism is exact.** *Mi ama · yu ama · kita ama* — three subjects, one
verb, not a letter different. English cannot do this: *I love, you love, she
loves*. Nor can Spanish, Arabic, Russian or Hindi, all of which bend the verb
to its subject. Amadunia's promise that no word ever changes shape turns out to
be a poetic resource and not only a learner's convenience, and this is the
first text to use it on purpose.

**Rhyme is cheap.** Half the three hundred roots end in a vowel and 23% end in
*-a* alone; *-ar* and *-an* close another 8% between them. A rhyme is always
available.

That is worth saying plainly rather than boasting about: **a rhyme that costs
nothing is worth little.** In a language where a quarter of all words end in
the same sound, rhyming is not a craft. If Amadunia ever grows a poetry, its
music will have to come from rhythm and repetition, not from endings.

## What the language could not do

1. **There is no rhythm, because stress has never been decided.**
   [phonology.md](../grammar/phonology.md) records that as open, and until now
   it was an abstraction. Read the first line aloud twice, stressing *DIN lai*
   and then *din LAI*: both are correct Amadunia, and they are different poems.
   Two readers cannot agree on the metre of a single line. **This is the gap
   that matters most for anything meant to be spoken**, and a poem is where it
   stops being theoretical.

2. **No word for "change".** The first stanza is about a day turning into a
   night and back, and the language cannot name what is happening. Already
   [recorded](text-3-lingua-ini.md), and felt harder here.

3. **Fragments once more.** *Din lai. Sol upar.* are full sentences, but a poem
   wants to break a line where it likes, and nothing says whether it may.

## Roots used

21 of 300 — the fewest of any text here, which is the point. A poem should be
sayable by someone three lessons in.
