# Mila tahun

*A thousand years.*

The tenth text. [Text 9](text-9-pagi-in-madina.md) was written from the
frequency list and emptied most of its tail; what stayed behind was all of one
kind — *amani*, *arte*, *historia*, *legis*, *natura*, *numero*, *sansi*,
*mati*, *proba*, *mila*. Peace, art, history, law, nature, number, chance, to
die, to try, a thousand.

Nine texts about tea, hospitals, letters and errands could not reach a single
one of them, and that is not a failure of those texts. Abstract words need an
argument, not a scene. This is the second text with an argument in it, after
[text 3](text-3-lingua-ini.md), and the argument is what a language shares with
a mountain.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 23.

---

```
Insan hidup sen tahun. Insan mati.
Pahar hidup lebi dari insan.
Pahar hidup mila tahun. Natura no mati.

Insan suda katab historia. Historia no mati.
Arte in dom eski. Kalima in libro eski.

Legis lai dari insan. Legis no lai dari natura.
Amani lai dari insan.

Numero no mati. Uan es uan in dunia.
Lingua mati kab insan stop sema.

Mi proba sema. Yu proba sema.
Kita punya sansi.
```

## What the text found

**Abstraction costs nothing extra.** *Legis lai dari insan* is four words and
so is *Mi kupi pan in market*. There is no register here, no formal vocabulary
and no longer forms for weightier things — the same seventeen rules carry a
shopping list and a claim about where law comes from. That is not an
achievement of this text; it is what having no conjugation and no articles
buys, and this is the first page where it can be seen plainly.

**Negation does the arguing.** Four of the seventeen sentences are *no*
sentences, and they are the spine of it: nature does not die, history does not
die, law does not come from nature, number does not die.
The language has one negator, it goes in one place, and it turns out to be
enough to build a case with.

**The last three sentences change person.** *Mi proba*, *Yu proba*, *Kita
punya* — I, you, we. The pronouns never change shape, so the shift is carried
by nothing but the words themselves, and the inclusive *kita* does the work
that in English needs a choice about whether "we" includes the reader.

## Gaps

**A gap claimed here on the first draft was not one.** The draft said the
language could not compare durations — that *lebi* scales adjectives and nouns
and nothing scales a verb — and [comparison.md](../grammar/comparison.md) has a
section headed *With verbs* saying exactly the opposite: the same words work on
a verb, in the adverb slot, *Mi kara lebi dari yu*. The text now says *Pahar
hidup lebi dari insan*, a mountain lives longer than a person, which is what it
wanted in the first place. A gap asserted without reading the rule page is not
a gap.

**It cannot say "for a thousand years".** *mila tahun* is a bare time
expression, and whether that means a point or a span is [the question recorded
in place.md](../grammar/place.md) about *in*. Here the span reading is the only
sensible one, which is evidence but not a decision.

**It has no word for "forever", and did not reach for one.** Worth writing down
that it did not: *no mati*, does not die, said what was wanted four times, and
a word for *forever* would have been used had it existed. That is the shape of
a gap the language has not yet noticed it has.
