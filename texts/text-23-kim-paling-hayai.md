# Kim paling hayai

*Who is the fastest.*

The twenty-third text, and the first written **for comparison** — which became
the thinnest rule the day before this page was written, when subordination
turned out to have a second shape and stopped being it. Comparison stood in six
texts of twenty-two, against place's twenty.

It is the fourth rule to be given a page of its own, and the shortest argument
for one: children rank things. *lebi*, *kurang* and *paling* say more, less and
most; *dari* is "than" and *kadar* is "as ... as". A conversation about which
animal is fastest uses all five without being made to.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 26.

---

```
Pagi. Anak-anak otur in bage. Ta-ta kan animal-animal.

Anak kabir sema: "Farasi paling kabir."
Anak keci respon: "No. Farasi kabir, aur pahar lebi kabir dari farasi."

Kanis hayai. Kucing hayai kadar kanis.
Farasi lebi hayai dari kanis.
Tair paling hayai porke tair in udara.

Samaki kurang kabir dari farasi.
Samaki hidup in sungai. Sungai lebi baid dari bage.

Anak keci sema: "Mi hayai kadar tair."
Dugu ta sema: "Yu kurang hayai dari kucing."
Anak keci kimbia. Kucing kimbia lebi hayai.
Anak-anak kanta.

Mualim lai aur tanya: "Kim paling hayai?"
Anak-anak respon: "Tair!"
Mualim sema: "Tair paling hayai in udara. Samaki paling hayai in akua."
Ta sema kanis kurang hayai dari farasi.
Ta sema kanis es rafiki lebi hao.

Aksam. Anak-anak go dom.
Anak keci fikir tair lebi hao dari kucing.
Dugu ta fikir kucing lebi hao dari tair.
Du anak senang.
```

## What comparison did

**Fourteen of the twenty-nine sentences compare something**, and between them
they use every part of the rule:

| | |
|---|---|
| lebi kabir, lebi hayai, lebi baid, lebi hao | more |
| kurang kabir, kurang hayai | less |
| paling kabir, paling hayai | most |
| dari farasi, dari kanis, dari bage, dari kucing, dari tair | than |
| kadar kanis, kadar tair | as ... as |

*Kucing kimbia lebi hayai* is the one that puts a degree word on a verb rather
than a noun — *lebi* in the adverb slot, scaling *hayai* where the adverb rule
puts it. The rule page states that shape in one line and the corpus had barely
used it.

The last three sentences are deliberate. Two children compare the same two
animals and reach opposite answers, and the language says both with the same
four words in the same order. **Nothing in a comparison marks whose it is** —
*lebi hao* is "better" and never "better to me" — so the two lines are not
contradictory, they are two people each stating a fact the language has no way
to qualify.

## Gaps

**"The biggest of the three" cannot be said.** *paling* names a superlative
without naming what it is superlative among, and
[the rule page records the gap](../grammar/comparison.md): *paling kabir dari
tri* is untested and was not tested here. The teacher's answer works around it
by naming the place instead — *paling hayai in udara* — which is a different
sentence and an honest one.

**No word for "than" that is not also "from".** *dari* does both jobs, which is
settled and deliberate — [the rule page argues it](../grammar/comparison.md) —
but this page puts the two jobs one line apart: *Samaki hidup in sungai* and
*Sungai lebi baid dari bage*. A reader meeting *dari* in the
second sentence has to know the first word after it is a standard and not a
source, and nothing but the *lebi* before it says so.

**No comparative for a noun against a noun without an adjective.** *A dog is
more of a friend than a cat* has no form: the page says *kanis es rafiki lebi
hao*, "the dog is a better friend", which needs an adjective to lean on.
[Recorded with the other gaps in comparison.md](../grammar/comparison.md).
