# Mama mama mi

*My mother's mother.*

The twenty-fourth text, and the first **portrait of one person**. The genres so
far are stories, arguments, dialogues, a poem, a letter, a recipe, a meal, a
room, a life and a journey; nobody had described a face.

It is also a test of something the gap list raises and does not settle. The
dictionary has no word for a grandmother, and
[the kinship set is fourteen of twenty-seven](../dictionary/a1-checklist.md) —
but possession puts the owner straight after the thing owned, so *mama mama mi*
is the mother of my mother and needs no root at all. This page is written to
find out whether that holds for a whole text or only for a heading.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 26.

---

```
Mama mama mi hidup in dom keci.
Dom ta in tarik. Sungai dekat.

Rambut ta putih. Ain ta keci aur hao.
Mano ta kuat porke ta suda hidup cok tahun.
Cehra ta punya cok historia.

Ta otur in kursi kab aksam lai.
Ta kara libro eski. Ta no mau telefon.

Mi lai. Ta sema: "Otur sini, anak."
Ta beri ca garam aur pan dulce.
Kita sema. Kita kanta.

Ta ingat tempo baid.
Ta sema: "Mi suda kan dunia."
Mi dengar. Mi no sema.

Ta suda hidup umur cang.
Ta punya cok sabar aur cok kalima.

Rat lai. Ta lala.
Mi kan cehra ta. Mi senang aur mi sedih.
```

## What the possession rule did

**It gave the language a grandmother without a root.** *mama mama mi* is two
applications of one rule: *mama mi* is my mother, and *mama* in front of that
phrase owns it. Nothing marks either step. The same shape gives a grandfather,
and *dugu mama mi* an uncle or an aunt — which is
[what the gap note already suspected](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for)
and what a whole page now shows.

**And it holds for exactly one step of remove.** *mama mama mama mi* would be a
great-grandmother, and it is unreadable rather than ungrammatical: three
identical words in a row with nothing to group them. The rule does not forbid
it and no reader could parse it, which is a different kind of limit from the
ones this repository usually records — not a missing word but a shape that runs
out.

## Gaps

**Nothing can be near anything.** The first draft of the second line read
*Dom ta in tarik dekat sungai* — her house is on a road near the river — and it
is not a sentence: *dekat* is an adjective, adjectives follow their noun, and
so *tarik dekat* is "a near road" with the river left dangling. The language
has three prepositions, *in*, *dari* and *por*, and none of them is *beside*.
The page says *Dom ta in tarik. Sungai dekat.* — two sentences, with the
relation between them left to the reader. [Recorded with the other things the
writing has asked for](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for),
and it is the first gap this repository has found in the
[preposition set](../grammar/place.md) rather than in the vocabulary.

**No word for an old person.** *eski* is old and is used here of a book. Using
it of a person is grammatical and cold; the text says *ta suda hidup umur
cang*, she has lived a long life, which is longer and kinder and not the same
thing. *genc* has an opposite in [the dictionary](../dictionary/dictionary.md)
only for objects.

**No word for *kind*.** *Ain ta keci aur hao* is glossed "small and kind"
because that is what the sentence means here, and *hao* is simply "good". The
language cannot separate a good person from a kind one, and this page leans on
context to do it. [Recorded with the other gaps](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for).

**A face cannot hold anything.** *Cehra ta punya cok historia* — her face has
much history — is the nearest the language gets to a face that shows a life.
*punya* is possession and nothing else; there is no *show*, no *carry*, no
*tell* that takes a thing rather than a person, and
[the verb list](../dictionary/dictionary.md) has no candidate to press into the
job.

**And the portrait has no word for what a portrait is about.** There is no
*look* as a noun, no *age* apart from *umur* which is also *life*, and no way
to say someone is beautiful in the sense a grandchild means. *sundar* is in
[the dictionary](../dictionary/dictionary.md) and is not used here, because it
would have been the wrong word.
