# Gusa in tarik

*Anger on the road.*

The eighteenth text, and the last of the sweep. Two days ago 55 roots had never
appeared in any text; [text 16](text-16-berapa-harga.md) took twenty-one and
[text 17](text-17-tren-aur-farasi.md) took twenty-seven. Five were left that
could be written: *burun*, *dekat*, *gusa*, *kiri*, *polisi*. They are all
here, in the one scene that holds them — a blocked street.

Two more remain and are not here on purpose. See the gaps below.

---

```
Madina in aksam. Cok auto in tarik.

Auto no anda. Sofer punya gusa.
Ta sema kuat: "Kaifa mi go dom?"

Polisi lai. Ta sema: "Sabar, plis."
Polisi suda funga tarik ini. Ta sema: "Go kiri."

Sofer tanya: "Market dekat?"
Polisi respon: "Ya, dekat. Pat momen."

Anak keci in auto. Burun ta barid.
Mama ta funga pencere.

Auto anda kiri. Tarik baru duan.
Sofer no punya gusa sasa.

Polisi anda in tarik tena.
```

## Line by line

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Madina in aksam. | The city in the evening. |
| Cok auto in tarik. | Many cars on the road. |
| Auto no anda. | The cars do not move. |
| Sofer punya gusa. | The driver has anger. |
| Ta sema kuat: "Kaifa mi go dom?" | He says strongly: "How do I get home?" |
| Polisi lai. | A police officer comes. |
| Ta sema: "Sabar, plis." | She says: "Patience, please." |
| Polisi suda funga tarik ini. | The police have closed this road. |
| Ta sema: "Go kiri." | She says: "Go left." |
| Sofer tanya: "Market dekat?" | The driver asks: "Is the market near?" |
| Polisi respon: "Ya, dekat. Pat momen." | She answers: "Yes, near. Four moments." |
| Anak keci in auto. | A small child is in the car. |
| Burun ta barid. | His nose is cold. |
| Mama ta funga pencere. | His mother closes the window. |
| Auto anda kiri. | The car goes left. |
| Tarik baru duan. | The new road is short. |
| Sofer no punya gusa sasa. | The driver has no anger now. |
| Polisi anda in tarik tena. | The officer walks the road again. |

## What it reached

The five: *burun*, *dekat*, *gusa*, *kiri*, *polisi*. **Every root in the
dictionary that can be written has now been written in a text**, and
[`check.py`](../check.py) holds that.

*gusa* is the interesting one. It is a noun — anger is something a person
*has*, the way *korku* is — so *Sofer punya gusa* and, at the end, *Sofer no
punya gusa sasa*. English would say he *was* angry and then *calmed down*;
Amadunia says he had a thing and then did not. The frame is the language's, not
a translation of ours, and it came out of the grammar rather than being
designed.

*dekat* stayed an adjective. *Market dekat?* is "is the market near?", a
predicate, and nowhere does this page write *dekat market* — that would be
[the preposition the language does not have](../grammar/place.md).

## Gaps

**Two roots are left and neither can be written.** *madad* — its class is
undecided, so a sentence using it would answer
[the open question](../grammar/verb-chains.md) by accident. *kadang* — where a
frequency adverb stands is [open](../grammar/adverbs.md), so it cannot appear
in a sentence without settling that.

This scene wanted both. *Polisi madad sofer*, the officer helps the driver, is
the sentence the story is actually about, and it cannot be written. *Kadang
tarik funga*, sometimes the road is closed, is what the officer would say.
Two open questions, two sentences, and the words sitting in the dictionary
unable to work.

**No word for *but*, a fifth time.** *Tarik baru duan* wants "the new road was
short **but** slow".
