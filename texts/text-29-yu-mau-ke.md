# Yu mau ke?

*What do you want?*

The twenty-ninth text, and the second rung on [the reading
ladder](../lessons/reading-ladder.md): **only the roots the lessons have taught
by Lesson 08**, and no rule beyond Lesson 07.

[Text 28](text-28-din-mi.md) opened the ladder at Lesson 06 with a day that
nobody speaks in. This is the other thing eight lessons buy: a conversation.
Lesson 07 gives the question words, and almost every line here is a question or
an answer to one.

Eight lessons in there is still no *aur*, no *no* before a predicate, no *es*,
no *ini* or *itu*, no preposition and no verb chain. A question, a possessor
and the two tense markers are the whole apparatus.

---

```
Mama mi go market. Mi go market.

— Hi! Yu mau ke?
— Mi mau pan.
— Berapa pan?
— Du pan.
— Des pesa.
— Mi beri des pesa. Mersi!

— Yu mau ca?
— Ya. Ca garam?
— Ya, ca garam.
— Mi mau ca. Mi mau akua barid.

Insan lai. Insan kan foto mi.
— Kim?
— Papa mi.
— Ta lai kab?
— Ta saufa lai aksam.

— Yu go nali?
— Mi go dom. Mama mi go dom.
— Bai!
— Bai!

Mi kula pan. Mi nomu ca.
Mama mi nomu akua.
Din hao.
```

## Line by line

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Mama mi go market. Mi go market. | My mother goes to the market. I go to the market. |
| — Hi! Yu mau ke? | — Hello! What do you want? |
| — Mi mau pan. | — I want bread. |
| — Berapa pan? | — How many breads? |
| — Du pan. | — Two breads. |
| — Des pesa. | — Ten money. |
| — Mi beri des pesa. Mersi! | — I give ten money. Thank you! |
| — Yu mau ca? | — Do you want tea? |
| — Ya. Ca garam? | — Yes. Is the tea hot? |
| — Ya, ca garam. | — Yes, the tea is hot. |
| — Mi mau ca. Mi mau akua barid. | — I want tea. I want cold water. |
| Insan lai. Insan kan foto mi. | A person comes. The person looks at my photo. |
| — Kim? | — Who? |
| — Papa mi. | — My father. |
| — Ta lai kab? | — When does he come? |
| — Ta saufa lai aksam. | — He will come in the evening. |
| — Yu go nali? | — Where are you going? |
| — Mi go dom. Mama mi go dom. | — I am going home. My mother is going home. |
| — Bai! — Bai! | — Goodbye! — Goodbye! |
| Mi kula pan. Mi nomu ca. | I eat bread. I drink tea. |
| Mama mi nomu akua. | My mother drinks water. |
| Din hao. | The day is good. |

## Two kinds of question, and both are here

*Ke*, *berapa*, *kim*, *kab* and *nali* stand exactly where the answer will
stand — **Yu mau ke?** is answered **Mi mau pan**, with the word swapped and
nothing else moved. That is the Lesson 07 rule and it is the reason this page
can be a conversation at all.

*Ca garam?* has no question word in it. It is a statement asked on a rising
tone, and the lessons have been doing that since Lesson 01 — which is why the
reading ladder counts the two separately, and why this text is dated by its
words rather than by its questions.

**A fragment answers most of them.** *Du pan*, *Des pesa*, *Papa mi*, *Kim* —
four turns with no verb in them. Nothing was added to allow that; a phrase may
stand alone [when the question makes it clear](../grammar/sentence-types.md).

## Gaps

**A price cannot be named, only counted.** *Des pesa* is "ten money", and the
shopkeeper who says it is doing arithmetic out loud. *harga*, price, arrives in
Lesson 21 — thirteen lessons after a learner can walk into a market and ask for
bread. [Text 16](text-16-berapa-harga.md) records the same gap at 300 roots for
a different reason: a price cannot be named *cheap* or *dear* either.

**Nothing can be asked with *kaifa*.** *How* is in
[Lesson 07](../lessons/lesson-07-questions.md) with the other five question
words and there is nothing here to ask it about: it wants a verb of making or
doing, and *suru*, to make, arrives in Lesson 22. The word is taught nine
lessons before the first sentence that can use it.

**Two turns cannot be joined and none can be denied.** *aur* and predicate
*no* are both [Lesson 14](../lessons/lesson-14-not-and.md), so the shopkeeper
cannot say *I have bread and tea* and cannot say *there is no tea*. Every
answer on this page is yes.

**And nobody can be asked who they are.** *Kim yu?* would need *es* to be
answered — *Mi es doktor* — and [the copula is Lesson 11](../grammar/copula.md).
*Kim* here can only ask about a person already in the room, which is why it
asks about a photograph.
