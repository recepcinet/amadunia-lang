# Surat por mama

*A letter to mother.*

The seventh text, and the first that is written to someone. The stories tell,
the third argues, the fourth is two people talking, the fifth is meant to be
said aloud. A letter is none of those: one person, absent, addressing another
person, absent, about a third place. It is the form that leans hardest on
tense and on the second person, and it turned out to be the form the language
is furthest from being able to finish.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 23.

---

```
Salam mama,

Mi in madina baru sasa. Dom mi keci aur safi.
Es pencere kabir in kamra mi.
Mi lihat asman dari pencere.

Insan-insan sini hao. Mi punya du rafiki baru.
Ta-ta studi in skola una mi.

Mi ingat dom kita. Mi ingat ca yu. Mi ingat golos papa.
Mi sedih porke yu-yu baid.

Mi suda anda por madina kemarin.
Mi suda lihat ponte cang aur pahar kabir.
Madina ini sundar. Aur ta no es dom mi.

Mi saufa lai dom. Mi espera din itu.
Mi hao sini. No es korku.

Anak yu
```

## What the text found

**The three tenses carry the whole letter.** *Mi suda anda* yesterday, *Mi
lihat* now, *Mi saufa lai* to come — three particles, no verb changing shape
once. A letter in English needs walked, walk, will walk, saw, see, will see;
this one needs *suda*, nothing, and *saufa*, and they never touch the verb.

**Negation does the work of a whole sentence twice.** *Aur ta no es dom mi* —
and it is not my home — is the turn the letter is built around, and *No es
korku*, there is no fear, is the reassurance it ends on. The existential *es*
and the negator *no* were settled separately and combine without a rule being
added for the combination.

## Gaps

Four things this letter wanted and could not have. None was invented.

**A letter cannot be signed.** *With love* has no form: *ama* is the verb to
love, and there is no noun for love. One root does one job, so the verb cannot
be borrowed as a noun the way English borrows it — that is the rule working as
designed, and the cost is that the most common closing in the world's
correspondence cannot be written. The letter signs *Anak yu*, your child,
which is a fact rather than a feeling.

**It cannot say "I miss you".** The nearest the language reaches is *Mi sedih
porke yu-yu baid*, I am sad because you are far, which is a paraphrase of the
cause, not the feeling. This is a vocabulary gap and joins the missing word
for pain that [text 6](text-6-seti-din.md) found. Both were found by writing,
which is now the second time that has happened and the first time it has
happened twice to the same kind of word — the ones about how a person feels.

**It cannot say "before I came" or "after I arrive".** A letter is made of
those. *although*, *before*, *after* and *until* are all on the open list, so
every sentence here had to be arranged so that no clause needed one. That is
why the middle of the letter is four short sentences instead of two long ones.

**It could not say "in the month I arrive" without answering a live
question.** Whether a plain time noun takes *in* was [only just recorded as
open](../grammar/place.md), so the letter says *Mi espera din itu*, I wait for
that day, and stays out of it.
