# Dom eski

*The old house.*

The twenty-sixth text, and the first **memory**. Everything in it happened, and
the language has one way to say so: *suda* in front of the verb, the same mark
for a thing that happened once and a thing that went on for years.

It was written to find out what that costs. A memory is made of the difference
between what was and what is, and Amadunia can say both — but it cannot say
that one thing had already finished when another began, which is the shape most
of a memory takes.

Written at 300 roots, with no grammar beyond Lesson 26.

---

```
Mi ingat dom eski.

Dom itu suda es dom familia mi.
Dom itu lebi keci dari dom mi sasa.
Bage itu lebi kabir.

Pohon cang suda hidup in bage.
Anak-anak suda kimbia in bage.
Mama suda tabak corba. Papa suda kanta.

Kamra mi keci aur barid.
Uan pencere. Uan lampu.
Mi suda lala kab luma suda mati.

Sasa dom itu no es dom mi.
Insan-insan baru hidup situ.
Pohon no situ.

Mi suda go tarik itu.
Mi suda kan pencere. Mi no suda kan anak-anak.

Dom mi baru lebi kabir aur lebi safi.
Mi senang in dom baru.
Aur mi sedih kab mi ingat dom eski.
```

## Line by line

|---|---|
| Mi ingat dom eski. | I remember the old house. |
| Dom itu suda es dom familia mi. | That house was my family's house. |
| Dom itu lebi keci dari dom mi sasa. | That house is smaller than my house now. |
| Bage itu lebi kabir. | That garden is bigger. |
| Pohon cang suda hidup in bage. | A tall tree lived in the garden. |
| Anak-anak suda kimbia in bage. | The children ran in the garden. |

| Papa suda kanta. | Father sang. |
| Kamra mi keci aur barid. | My room is small and cold. |
| Uan pencere. Uan lampu. | One window. One lamp. |
| Mi suda lala kab luma suda mati. | I slept when the light had died. |

| Pohon no situ. | The tree is not there. |
| Mi suda go tarik itu. | I went to that road. |
| Mi suda kan pencere. | I saw the window. |
| Mi no suda kan anak-anak. | I did not see the children. |
| Dom mi baru lebi kabir aur lebi safi. | My new house is bigger and cleaner. |
| Mi senang in dom baru. | I am happy in the new house. |
| Aur mi sedih kab mi ingat dom eski. | And I am sad when I remember the old house. |

## What one past marker does

*suda* is in nine of these twenty sentences and it is the same word every time.
*Papa suda kanta* is one evening; *Pohon cang suda hidup in bage* is thirty
years. The language does not distinguish them and this page does not try: the
difference is carried by what is being talked about, which is how Chinese,
Indonesian and Malay carry it too.

**The comparisons are what make it a memory rather than a list.** *Dom itu lebi
keci dari dom mi sasa* puts then beside now in one sentence — and the tense
marker is on neither half, because a comparison of an adjective has no verb to
mark. The old house is smaller *now*, in the remembering, which is not quite
what the sentence means and is exactly what it says.

## Gaps

**Nothing can have already happened.** *Mi suda lala kab luma suda mati* is
"I slept when the light died", and the sentence wants *had died* — one past
inside another. Both verbs take the same *suda* and nothing orders them.
[The tense page](../grammar/tense.md) settles that the verb never changes, so
this is not a missing word but the price of that decision, and this is the
first page to pay it in full.

**No *before*, no *after*, no *until*.** Each is
[open](../grammar/README.md) and none was invented here. A memory is mostly
made of those words, and the page is written round them: every sentence stands
on its own and the order they are printed in does the work.

**No *ago*, and no way to say how long.** *tahun ini* is this year and *kemarin*
is yesterday, and between them there is nothing — no *ten years ago*, no *for a
long time*. The tree lived in the garden and the page cannot say for how long,
which is the sentence a memory most wants. [Recorded with the other things the
writing has asked for](../dictionary/README.md#words-the-writing-has-asked-for).

**An adjective predicate cannot be put in the past at all.** *Kamra mi keci*
is "my room is small", and the sentence this page wanted is *my room was
small*. There is nowhere to put *suda*: it goes in front of a verb, and an
adjective predicate has no verb — [that is the copula rule, and it is
deliberate](../grammar/copula.md). Writing *Kamra mi suda es keci* is the
obvious repair and [the checker refuses it](../check.py), because *es* never
stands before an adjective. So the room is described in the present inside a
page that is entirely past, and the reader is left to carry the tense across
from the sentences around it. This is the sharpest thing this page found and it
is not a missing word: it is what *no conjugation* costs, in the one place the
cost is not free.
