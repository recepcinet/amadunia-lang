# Din mi

*My day.*

The twenty-eighth text, and the new lowest rung on [the reading
ladder](../lessons/reading-ladder.md): it uses **only the roots the lessons
have taught by Lesson 06**, and no rule beyond that lesson.

Until it was written the ladder began at Lesson 10, which meant nine lessons of
teaching before there was anything to read. This page was written for those
nine.

Six lessons in, there is no *aur*, no *no*, no question word, no *es*, no
preposition, no *ini* or *itu*, no adverb, no comparison and no clause inside a
clause. What is there is a subject, a verb, an object, an adjective after its
noun, an adjective standing as the whole predicate, the plural, possession, the
two tense markers and the numbers. Everything below is made of that.

---

```
Sol lai. Pagi hao.

Mama mi lai. Mama mi beri ca.
Mi nomu ca. Ca hao.

Papa mi kula pan.
Anak keci nomu akua.

Sol kabir. Din kabir.
Mi go. Rafiki mi go.
Kita kan dunia. Dunia kabir.

Rafiki mi sema. Mi sema.

Aksam lai. Sol keci.
Papa mi lai dom.
Kita kula pan. Kita nomu ca.

Rat lai. Dom mi keci.
Anak-anak keci. Mi ama anak-anak.

Mi ama mama mi. Mi ama papa mi.
Mi ama rafiki-rafiki mi.

Sol suda go. Sol saufa lai besok.
Din hao.
```

## Line by line

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Sol lai. Pagi hao. | The sun comes. The morning is good. |
| Mama mi lai. Mama mi beri ca. | My mother comes. My mother gives tea. |
| Mi nomu ca. Ca hao. | I drink tea. The tea is good. |
| Papa mi kula pan. | My father eats bread. |
| Anak keci nomu akua. | The small child drinks water. |
| Sol kabir. Din kabir. | The sun is big. The day is big. |
| Mi go. Rafiki mi go. | I go. My friend goes. |
| Kita kan dunia. Dunia kabir. | We see the world. The world is big. |
| Rafiki mi sema. Mi sema. | My friend speaks. I speak. |
| Aksam lai. Sol keci. | Evening comes. The sun is small. |
| Papa mi lai dom. | My father comes home. |
| Kita kula pan. Kita nomu ca. | We eat bread. We drink tea. |
| Rat lai. Dom mi keci. | Night comes. My house is small. |
| Anak-anak keci. Mi ama anak-anak. | The children are small. I love the children. |
| Mi ama mama mi. Mi ama papa mi. | I love my mother. I love my father. |
| Mi ama rafiki-rafiki mi. | I love my friends. |
| Sol suda go. Sol saufa lai besok. | The sun has gone. The sun will come tomorrow. |
| Din hao. | The day is good. |

## What six lessons buy

Every sentence here is two or three words, and that is not a style. **No
sentence can be joined to another**, because *aur* arrives in Lesson 14, so
*Mi nomu ca. Ca hao.* is two sentences where one was meant. **Nothing can be
denied**, because *no* before a predicate is Lesson 14 as well: the day in this
text is good throughout, and it could not have been otherwise. **Nothing can be
asked**, because the question words arrive in Lesson 07. And **nothing is
anywhere**: *in*, *dari* and *por* are Lesson 15, so the father comes home and
the family is never *in* the house.

What is left turns out to be a day. The three time words — *pagi*, *aksam*,
*rat* — carry the whole shape of it without a preposition or a marker between
them, standing as the subject of a sentence of their own: *Aksam lai*, evening
comes.

**The sun does the work a conjunction would.** *Sol lai* opens it, *Sol kabir*
is the middle of the day, *Sol keci* is the evening and *Sol suda go* is the
end. A reader six lessons in has no word that means *then*, so the sun is the
only thing that can say it.

## Gaps

**Five adjectives, and the tea cannot be hot.** Everything describable by
Lesson 06 is describable as *hao*, *mal*, *kabir*, *keci* or *cok* — good, bad,
big, small, many. *garam*, hot, is Lesson 08. The first draft of this page
wrote *Pagi luma* for *the morning is bright* three times, and
[`check.py`](../check.py) rejected all three: *luma* is the noun **light**, so
the line said *morning light* and had no predicate in it at all. There is no
adjective for bright at any lesson, so the line is *Pagi hao* now — which is
[the greeting](../phrasebook.md), and the next gap is about that.

**A noun cannot be a predicate here.** *My mother is a doctor* needs *es*, and
[*es* arrives in Lesson 11](../lessons/lesson-11-being.md) — the word is met
earlier and the sentence pattern is not. The one thing a person most wants to
say when introducing a family is the one thing this rung cannot say.

**And *Pagi hao* is both a sentence and a greeting.** *The morning is good*
and *Good morning!* are the same three syllables with nothing to tell them
apart; [copula.md records it](../grammar/copula.md) as a decision the language
has not made. A page at this rung cannot write around it, because the only
other bright-morning adjective is *kabir*.
