# In skola

*At school.*

The twentieth text, written for the gap between Lesson 13 and Lesson 18 on
[the reading ladder](../lessons/reading-ladder.md). It uses **only the 136
roots the lessons have taught by Lesson 16**, which is the lesson called
*School and time* — so a learner who has just finished it reads a page about
what it just taught.

No verb chains, no existential *es*, no *porke* joining two clauses, no
comparison of adjectives: all four arrive in Lessons 17 and 18.

---

```
Pagi. Anak-anak go skola.

Skola in tarik ini. Skola kabir.
Anak-anak otur in kursi.

Mi buka libro mi. Mi kara.
Rafiki mi katab in libro ta.

Mi no faham. Mi sema: "Kaifa?"
Rafiki mi sema: "Kan ini."
Mi kan. Sasa mi faham.

Hora du. Kita kula pan in skola.
Anak-anak kimbia in tarik.

Aksam. Mi go dom.
Mi kara libro tena. Mi studi.
Papa mi sema: "Yu studi hao."

Mi mau libro baru. Pesa mi keci.
Besok mi saufa go skola tena.
```

## What sixteen lessons are enough for

Twenty-one sentences, 42 roots. A day at school told with the plain sentence:
subject, verb, object, an adjective after its noun, a preposition, a
demonstrative, the plural, possession, a command, a question and the two tense
markers. Nothing here is a workaround; it is simply a smaller language, and
the smaller language is the one the learner has at that point.

**One sentence carries the whole of the missing grammar.** *Mi no faham. Mi
sema: "Kaifa?"* is two sentences because at Lesson 16 there is no *porke* to
join them — *I did not understand, so I asked how* has to be said twice. Every
other absence in this page is the same shape: the rule arrives one or two
lessons later.

## Gaps

**A school with no teacher.** *mualim* is not taught until Lesson 19, so the
only people in this school are children and a friend. Lesson 16 is called
*School and time* and gives the building, the book and the chair; the person
who teaches is filed in Lesson 19 among doors, windows, trees and animals.
Whether that word should move is a syllabus question and this page does not
answer it — it records that a school text written at Lesson 16 cannot put a
teacher in the room.

Nothing else was wanted that the language lacks: the vocabulary of a school
day at 136 roots is enough for a school day.
