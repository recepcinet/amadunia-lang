# Anak espera sol

*The child waits for the sun.*

An original story, written with 34 of Amadunia's 113 roots and no grammar beyond Lesson 13.

---

```
Din. Anak keci otur. Sol upar.
Sol kabir. Sol garam. Sol beri luma.

Anak kan upar. Anak sema: "Sol, yu es kim?"
Sol beri luma.

Anak sema: "Yu es rafiki mi?"
Sol beri luma. Din cang. Sol beri luma.

Rat lai. Sol go. Dunia barid.
Anak espera. Anak espera cok.
Angin lai. Yuki lai. Anak keci sini.

Din baru lai. Sol lai upar.
Anak kimbia. Anak sema: "Salam! Mi suda espera yu!"
Sol beri luma.

Anak faham: rafiki go, rafiki lai.
Anak otur. Anak kan sol. Anak hao.
```

## Line by line

| Amadunia | English |
|---|---|
| Din. Anak keci otur. Sol upar. | Day. A small child sits. The sun is above. |
| Sol kabir. Sol garam. Sol beri luma. | The sun is big. The sun is hot. The sun gives light. |
| Anak kan upar. Anak sema: "Sol, yu es kim?" | The child looks up. The child says: "Sun, who are you?" |
| Sol beri luma. | The sun gives light. |
| Anak sema: "Yu es rafiki mi?" | The child says: "Are you my friend?" |
| Sol beri luma. Din cang. Sol beri luma. | The sun gives light. The day is long. The sun gives light. |
| Rat lai. Sol go. Dunia barid. | Night comes. The sun goes. The world is cold. |
| Anak espera. Anak espera cok. | The child waits. The child waits a long time. |
| Angin lai. Yuki lai. Anak keci sini. | Wind comes. Snow comes. The small child is here. |
| Din baru lai. Sol lai upar. | A new day comes. The sun comes above. |
| Anak kimbia. Anak sema: "Salam! Mi suda espera yu!" | The child runs. The child says: "Hello! I waited for you!" |
| Sol beri luma. | The sun gives light. |
| Anak faham: rafiki go, rafiki lai. | The child understands: friends go, friends come. |
| Anak otur. Anak kan sol. Anak hao. | The child sits. The child looks at the sun. The child is well. |

## What the language did well

**The sun never answers in words, and it did not need to.** *Sol beri luma* repeated four times carries the whole weight of the story. A language with no way to say "the sun replied warmly" has to find another way, and repetition — which every oral tradition on Earth uses — was there waiting.

**Nothing had to be invented.** Every sentence is subject-verb-object, adjectives follow their noun, *es* appears only before a noun, and *suda* appears exactly once, where the child looks back at the waiting. The rules held for a whole story without a single exception.

## What the language could not say

Writing this found four gaps that thirteen lessons had not:

1. **No "and".** *Angin lai. Yuki lai.* should be one sentence — "wind and snow came". There is no conjunction in the language at all, so every list becomes a series of short sentences. This is the largest gap; it shapes the prose more than anything else.

2. **No "not".** The story has no negation in it because none is possible. "The sun did not answer" cannot be written, so the sun's silence had to be shown by what it did instead.

3. **No "again".** *Rafiki lai* is "friends come"; "friends come back" needs a word the language does not have. *baru* (new) was tried and rejected — *lai baru* reads as "comes new".

4. **No place marking.** *Anak kan sol* is "the child sees the sun" and "the child looks at the sun" alike; *Sol lai upar* leans on word order alone to mean "the sun comes up". Already noted in [Lesson 13](../lessons/lesson-13-weather-directions.md).

Gaps 1 and 2 are grammar decisions for the founder. Gaps 3 and 4 are partly vocabulary.
